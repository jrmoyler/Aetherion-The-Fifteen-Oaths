## Kiln v8b

**Narrow improvement observed:** the large exposed crown opening visible in the displayed frozen v7 is reduced; side/crown coverage is denser. This is a partial correction, not overall visual approval.

**Final likeness: FAIL. Priorities:**

1. **Eyes remain the clearest unresolved facial defect.** Profile still shows a conspicuous white eye protruding in front of the orbital area. Front eyes look flat and wide-open relative to the source, with weak wrapping lids and orbital depth. Correct globe seating, eyelid wrap and canthi before further surface polish. The extra front eye opening does not resolve the profile error.
2. **Hair trades a crown hole for a patterned cap.** Front fringe forms repeated thick descending locks with similar bends, and a broad smooth forehead/scalp cap remains visible. In profile, posterior/nape strands hang as separated straight pointed strips with visible gaps. The original has connected overlapping masses with irregular finer curls and directional variation. Build the whole side/back mass and primary lock flow before inserting more individual strips. Do not declare v8b uniformly closer than v7.
3. **Face silhouette remains simplified.** Blunt nasal tip, projecting lip mound, broad smooth cheek/jaw and polygon-rim ear still miss the source anatomy. Reduce the pout and shape bridge/alar/philtrum/chin transitions as one surface. Do not derive precise anatomical ratio claims from unmatched framing or the collar-obscured chin.

Acceptance target: front and profile jointly resolve eye containment and natural facial planes; hair must read as a layered connected hairstyle in both views, without large cap exposure or repeated hanging spikes. These two head diagnostics do not establish full body, masked face, gait, cape clearance or equipped grip acceptance. Preserve previously corrected cape/bracer asymmetry.

## Fault v11

**Narrow improvements observed:** crystal exposure is less uniformly broad than v10, actual shell depth is thinner in profile, and leather appears warmer/browner. These changes are visible, but source fidelity remains mixed.

**Final likeness: FAIL. Priorities:**

1. **Avoid replacing the uniform strip with a uniform narrow crack.** V11’s crystal aperture now pinches into a thin connected zigzag through large broad stone cells. The source presents a substantial irregular crystal interior with local occlusion, layered openings, darker depth and bright interlocking facets. Recover that changing volume and width instead of simply increasing stone coverage. The direction of the repair is partially useful; its present balance is wrong.
2. **Author fracture hierarchy in three dimensions.** Front remains dominated by broad, relatively flat stones arranged along the blade. Profile exposes long near-parallel slab edges around the dark/purple center. Introduce deliberate variations in overlap, thickness and fracture direction across large/medium/small scales; do not fix this with fine surface noise alone. Preserve the coherent blade outline while breaking the rail-like depth construction.
3. **Guard and pommel remain generic.** The guard reads as a stacked cylindrical/collar assembly rather than the reference’s fitted irregular bronze-and-stone enclosure. The pommel still centers one long prism inside a few shell pieces instead of the source’s compact fractured cluster. These silhouette/construction differences precede texture finish.
4. **Grip still has repeated broad wrap rhythm.** Warmer color helps, but construction remains regular. Match source wrap overlap and seam depth; then tune edge wear and roughness.

Acceptance target: matched front/profile plus tight guard/pommel detail should establish irregular coherent shell construction, crystal volume and fitted hardware before approval. No diagnosis of gamma corruption is supported. Static equipment does not need an artificial deformation rig to meet a count.
