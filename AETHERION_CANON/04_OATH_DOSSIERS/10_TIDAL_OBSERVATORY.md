## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Understanding the whole is valuable only when local lives still count. Observatory culture celebrates disciplined curiosity, yet its models can treat people as negligible errors when elegant predictions conflict with lived consequences.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed navigators studied celestial and gravitational disturbances after the Fracture. The shared word Tidal names periodic attraction here, while the Water Covenant uses it for material flow; the historical explanation remains a proposal.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Research circles publish models to a common review forum, while inhabited platform councils approve experiments affecting their homes. The institutional flaw is unequal authority between prestigious theorists and the people carrying practical risk.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Iria Deyn publishes their model's uncertainty and opposes rushing an experiment, while political sponsors demand a decisive forecast. Their flaw is withdrawing into methodological caution when a provisional decision is unavoidable; the player must help them act responsibly without pretending uncertainty can be eliminated.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Cal Ro maintains ballast systems and knows where the celebrated models fail in daily use. Navigators, instrument polishers and displaced platform residents provide evidence that technical expertise has several legitimate forms.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ballast Haven sits on stable mainland rock beneath the research platforms. It houses maintenance families and visitors who cannot tolerate altered gravity, keeping accessibility and ordinary life central to the cosmic realm.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Parallax Crown occupies an eastern headland with anchored observatories and limited suspended platforms. The platforms do not form a separate island realm; redundant mainland stairs and lifts preserve the continent's connected geography.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] High northeast cliffs overlook open sea and connect Reed, Theatre and the Court's eastern valleys. Dry clear nights alternate with coastal fog; good observation sites are a geographical resource rather than universal sky access.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Balanced cantilevers, broad instrument bearings and visible ballast masses frame controlled imbalance. Floating structures require legible anchoring, safe shutdown and occupied-space clearance, not decorative impossibility everywhere.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Indigo surfaces, starstone, orbital rings and aged metal match recovered reference traits. Matte black sighting baffles contrast with pale calibration marks; only selected anomalies bend the apparent horizon.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ballast-wing roostlings nest around stable thermal updrafts and avoid shifting gravity fields. Their movements warn workers of local anomalies, giving ecology an observational role without claiming animals understand celestial mathematics.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Coastal fog, strong sea wind and local gravity distortions define hazards. Anomalies have bounded volumes and safe transition zones, with motion-sickness options and a conventional-ground route through essential services.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Public sky evenings share discoveries in accessible language, while ballast crews host practical repair schools. Civic duty is to disclose uncertainty and compensate affected communities, not to accept every experiment for the sake of progress.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Defenders control approach vectors and stabilize critical platforms before using force. Strategic forecasts aid logistics, but celestial perspective cannot justify indiscriminate bombardment or treat civilian districts as expendable experimental terrain.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Gravity combat emphasizes telegraphed vectors, movement choice and clear landing outcomes. Meteor imagery requires readable target areas; lore does not turn a visual projectile into unlimited physical simulation or world-scale impact damage.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Counterweighted staffs, ring-mounted focuses and compact anchor blades show balanced mass. Moving rings stay clear of hands and faces, with visible locked travel states to distinguish instruments from generic magical jewelry.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Asymmetric weighted harnesses, stable boots and instrument-bearing shoulder pieces reflect variable load. Researchers and platform guards remain visually distinct; armor cannot rely on unexplained antigravity to ignore every joint or weight.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers calibrate local fields and test models against independent measurements. Their obligation is to publish limits and stop unsafe trials, making intellectual humility a practical exercise of power rather than an aesthetic trait.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth observers use blind angles, ballast service shafts and low-reflection cloth. They evade instruments with specific limitations, while defenders use custody records and ordinary observation instead of infallible cosmic sight.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include unauthorized experiment crews, unmoored constructs and wildlife displaced by field shifts. Research errors can create danger without making curiosity evil; deliberate concealment distinguishes culpable choices from honest uncertainty.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Unmoored Atlas is a living gravity-adapted colonial organism migrating through an experimental field that researchers mistakenly treated as empty space. Its passage threatens platforms, but tethering or destroying it damages an unknown ecology. Players stabilize residents, revise the model and negotiate an expensive relocation or altered research corridor.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ballast platforms follow marked corridors with fallback landings and passenger restraints. A low-gravity morph is a proposed temporary adaptation with explicit jump limits and camera comfort, never unrestricted flight or universal fall immunity.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Navigation, lens grinding, precision surveying, ballast engineering and public science education support civilian play. Reproducing a reliable modest measurement can matter more than discovering a grand but unusable theory.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Observatory exports navigation and measurement services while importing food, metals and maintenance labor. Funding tied to spectacular results can distort research priorities, giving replication and local safety visible political value.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Homes sit mostly on stable terraces, with optional anchored observation decks. Gravity customization is bounded inside housing instances and cannot alter public paths, guests' controls or neighboring plots without clear consent.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Scholarly standing measures reproducible work; local trust measures responsible experiments. A correct prediction obtained through undisclosed harm can raise prestige while damaging the alliance needed to use the knowledge well.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tidal navigators test celestial models at sea; Storm shares distributed observations; Fault certifies loads. Each partner supplies knowledge the Observatory lacks, preventing a cosmic faction from simply knowing everything better.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Escapement disputes causality assumptions, Daybreak challenges acceptable risk and Reed defends experiential testimony. These conflicts are not science versus superstition; they concern scope, uncertainty, authority and who bears errors.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A meteor-watch event requires forecasts, shelter preparation and safe recovery of fragments. Players distinguish predicted impact corridors from uncertain ones, and success is measured in protected lives and infrastructure rather than spectacular destruction.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Parallax puzzles compare viewpoints, shadow lengths and movable weights with diagrams and text alternatives. Gravity changes are deliberate and reversible, with camera-stable modes and no mandatory disorienting horizon rotation.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Falling Meridian crosses instrument halls and evacuated platform segments around a failing calibration system. Multiple routes use conventional lifts or Oath-assisted shortcuts, preserving first-Oath independence and accessibility.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A gravitational aggregation drifts toward occupied terraces during a field failure. The public encounter stabilizes anchor points and evacuation lanes; its scale is an authored hazard volume, not an unbounded celestial body simulation.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Gravity can redistribute loads in tagged puzzles, stabilize cargo or reveal a false surface. All interactions preserve explicit mass and location constraints in gameplay contracts; altered force cannot silently bypass ownership or PvP protections.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest chooses independent safety review, distributed research or emergency centralized prediction under a sunset charter. Every branch trades speed, coordination and capture risk while preserving public access to essential navigation.

## 35. Side stories
