## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Water is received, used and passed onward. Covenant culture values adaptable agreements, but inherited obligations can become ownership disguised as gratitude when a community cannot survive without another's channels.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] After the Fracture, proposed coastal stewards linked tide shelters and freshwater channels through reciprocal maintenance promises. The word Tidal refers here to terrestrial water exchange, distinct from the Observatory's gravitational use.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Watershed assemblies give upstream and downstream settlements a voice; harbor houses manage navigation separately. Drought arbitration requires public flow records so maritime wealth cannot silently overrule drinking-water needs.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Elen Voss openly defends harbor relief debts because those revenues fund storm protection and reliable food imports. They may accept cancellation, but only after confronting who will fund the replacement; their personal stake is the crews who trusted their guarantees, not simply a secret family fortune.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kavi Sen tends a public spring whose water is safe but whose access papers are not. Canal cleaners, navigators, midwives and boatwrights challenge the idea that temple officials alone sustain life.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Brackensluice sits where fresh and salt water mix, protected by staged gates rather than a single miraculous wall. Its residents know how upstream choices alter fishing, crops and foundation stability.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Confluence Quay is a mainland estuary city with shore-attached reef districts and navigable canals. Reef-city imagery never creates disconnected realm islands; occupied reefs connect by maintained causeways and accessible ferries.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The southwest coast receives Graft and Bitterglass drainage and western snowmelt. Freshwater sources, brackish marshes and open seawater remain distinct resources; an ocean view does not solve potable-water shortage.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Porous limestone arcades, glazed channel walls and removable flood floors embrace changing water levels. Public quays include accessible ramps and dry refuge levels; living aqueduct growth needs pruning and inspection.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Shell-lime plaster, water-worn stone, glazed ceramic and salt-stained timber show circulation and repair. Reef structures use cultivated or dead material, avoiding an unstated economy built on limitless reef destruction.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Reedback grazers keep channels open and shelter fish nurseries. Overgrazing or exclusion damages habitat differently, giving husbandry and fishing meaningful seasonal responsibilities beyond harvesting a decorative coastal biome.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Warm marine air brings seasonal rain, storm surge and brackish intrusion. Tide calendars are forecasts with uncertainty, and evacuation routes remain overland when magical water control fails.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Public washing courts, shared cooking water and boat repair shape everyday encounters. Obligation is a measurable maintenance share with hardship relief; hereditary debt is a contested institution, never a natural law of the element.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Defenders control channels, rescue routes and harbor approaches. Naval power can protect trade or enforce abusive tolls, while freshwater sabotage is a severe political violation with emergency mitigation built into event design.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Water combat emphasizes redirection, spacing, support and readable flow. Healing is not unlimited resource creation; audit source behavior separately from narrative hydrology, and preserve clear warning boundaries for wave effects.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Boat hooks, channel staffs and broad rescue knives have practical deck uses. Ceremonial weapons use replaceable shell-lime inlays and rope grips, with no assumption that water shapes remain rigid without magical support.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Flexible overlapping scales and waxed textiles drain quickly, with flotation and rescue variants. Heavy naval guards and spring healers share materials but differ in silhouette, avoiding one universal aquatic armor set.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers are spring conservators and surge readers as well as healers. Their ritual requires recording where water came from and where it will go, making a lifesaving diversion accountable to downstream people.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth crews inspect submerged sluices and move through maintenance culverts. Rebreathing equipment and visible access grates bound the fantasy; ordinary players retain ways to detect tampering without owning a Water Oath.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include predatory toll crews, failed aqueduct grafts and animals displaced by salinity changes. Water spirits have local ecological motives; the continent's rivers are not populated solely by hostile combat encounters.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Debt Leviathan is a living estuary guardian whose seasonal nursery migration blocks the port's food route. It is neither a debt-collecting machine nor malicious; relocation harms its young, closure threatens supplies, and a negotiated seasonal corridor requires expensive changes to shipping and habitat protection.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tidal skimmers work in marked shallow channels and require dry-land portage at breaks. A current-form morph is proposed for designated water paths, with surface exit safety and no universal travel through sealed pipes.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Hydraulics, boatbuilding, fishing, spring care, ceramic lining and tidal forecasting provide civilian advancement. Skilled channel cleaning can be as important to prosperity as rare maritime treasure.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tidal exports transport and water-management expertise while importing grain, timber and tools. Water allocation is not a simple auction; drought policy weighs household need, ecological flow and economically vital irrigation.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Homes have floodable ground utility floors and protected living levels. Canal plots include maintenance easements, and event damage changes repair tasks rather than permanently deleting stored furniture or blocking residents from their property.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Harbor standing measures fulfilled agreements; watershed trust measures fair passage of water. Paying every toll may please merchants while reinforcing harmful debt, so reputation exposes the tradeoff rather than assigning a single moral score.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Graft maintains upstream soils, Bitterglass tests contaminants, and Kiln supplies pumps and ceramics. Shared gauges make disputes auditable; cooperation does not mean accepting either partner's claim to unrestricted extraction.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Impulse demand strains industrial water; Sanguine's hereditary obligations echo contested harbor debt. Observatory navigation aids are welcome, but coastal workers reject celestial models that ignore local currents and lived uncertainty.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A salt-front event threatens freshwater intakes. Players relocate nurseries, repair gates and negotiate temporary use reductions; successful defenses can differ without permanently draining another realm or removing its core services.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sluice puzzles track source, height and salinity with labeled channels and tactile-style icons. A reset basin catches failed routing, avoiding an invisible puzzle mistake that floods a persistent player neighborhood.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Undertide Ledger combines submerged service halls with archives of relief contracts. Air refuges and non-swimming alternatives preserve access; recovered water VFX do not establish underwater locomotion as implemented.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A displaced estuary guardian shelters in a shipping channel during a surge. The event balances safe passage, nursery preservation and cargo rescue, and can end in relocation rather than compulsory slaughter.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Water can cool, clean, transport and reveal designated traces; it can also spread contamination if carelessly used. Effects are bounded to authored drainage volumes with visible recovery, never an infinite fluid simulation promise.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest renegotiates relief debts through cancellation with replacement funding, capped repayment or community service chosen by debtors. Every branch must maintain water access and acknowledge who bears the transition cost.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kavi's spring case tests whether undocumented residents count as beneficiaries. A boatwright asks for help rebuilding a ferry that competes with the capital's lucrative bridge toll, connecting small work to regional politics.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Water plus Poison can diagnose and treat or conceal contamination; Water plus Earth can protect a bank or deprive a wetland. Consent and watershed evidence govern the action rather than a universal element matchup.

## 37. Sound

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Overlapping channel rhythms, low dock percussion and human work songs anchor the sound. The tide-warning pattern is distinct from Observatory tones, reinforcing the two institutions' separate identities.

## 38. Visual motifs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Interlocking open channels, asymmetric knots and descending terraces express reciprocal flow. The emblem is an open bowl with an outward channel, not a crescent orbit or celestial tide symbol.

## 39. Palette and materials
