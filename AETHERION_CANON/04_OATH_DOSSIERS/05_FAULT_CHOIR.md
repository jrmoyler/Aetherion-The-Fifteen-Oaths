## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A promise should bear weight, but a structure survives by admitting movement. Choir citizens debate whether fidelity means preserving exact forms or keeping people safe when conditions make the old form fail.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed Fracture survivors stabilized collapsed passes by combining measured stonework and communal resonance. Their emergency standards hardened into a constitutional metaphor: every citizen assigned a bearing role in the social structure.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Terrace councils govern locally, while a load assembly certifies changes affecting shared foundations. Appeals require independent surveys, creating conflict when the institution that defines safe evidence also controls survey training.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Deren Korr is a patient structural arbiter who has prevented catastrophic collapses. They oppose a needed resettlement because admitting a historic design error would undermine the standards that currently keep other towns safe.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Pella Ust is a quarry surveyor mapping a fault beneath their childhood hall. Masons, lift operators, miners and cave growers give distinct accounts of the difference between honoring ancestors and repeating their mistakes.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Talus Steps is a movable terrace settlement outside old canyon walls. Its modular houses embarrass traditional elites but offer a practical model for living with unstable geology instead of pretending all movement is failure.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Keystone Hall occupies a broad canyon shoulder anchored to surveyed bedrock. Deep chambers are civic workspaces, not an excuse to remove ventilation, drainage, emergency exits or accessible vertical transport.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The central-west mountain spine separates wet western catchments from the interior basin. Passes and underground service routes connect several realms, while quarry spoil and blocked drainage create measurable downstream consequences.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Compression arches, buttressed galleries and deliberately separated movement joints make endurance visible. Crystal appears in surveyed seams and replaceable resonators, not as unsupported sharp geometry holding every roof.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Limestone, granite, layered shale and irregular crystal veins establish geological variation. Chisel patterns and settlement gauges show where forces travel; materials do not all have identical indestructible stone properties.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Resonance moles aerate thin soils and react to machinery vibrations. Cave fungi support food production but depend on ventilation and waste management, preventing the underground realm from becoming a consequence-free pantry.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Rain-shadow slopes are dry, canyon bottoms flash-flood, and fault movements disrupt foundations. Seismic warnings combine sound, gauge movement and visual notices so deaf players can participate in rescue and puzzles.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Public maintenance days repair paths and exchange grievances around common load maps. Civic obligation is contributing to shared structural safety; caste-like inherited assignments are a contested abuse of that principle.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Military crews fortify supply corridors and create fallback terraces rather than walling every border. Defenders must leave relief access and keep geological interventions within surveyed zones, preserving civilian escape during siege.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Earth combat rewards planned positioning and visible commitment. Raised cover and crystal zones need readable footprints, finite lifetimes and traversal counterplay; regional lore does not authorize permanent terrain edits from ordinary castables.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Counterweighted pole hammers, wedge shields and survey-pick sidearms combine construction and combat. Heavy heads require believable leverage, carrying systems and alternate civilian configurations.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Broad articulated stone-laminate plates sit on textile load harnesses with protected joints. Workers wear lighter dust and impact gear; no faction member is forced into a walking boulder silhouette to read as Earth.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers listen for stress through tuning frames and map it with chalk. Their obligation is to distinguish measurement from conviction, and falsified resonance readings carry civic consequences beyond magical backlash.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth specialists navigate inspection galleries and use vibration-damping soles. They bypass institutions through records and maintenance access; defenders find moved dust, altered gauge marks and missing wedges as concrete clues.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include illicit tunnel crews, damaged excavation constructs and territorial burrowers. Mining disputes allow noncombat settlements where safe access and livelihood can coexist, rather than casting all extraction as wicked.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Buried Adjudicator is a resonant verdict mechanism applying obsolete load allocations to occupied districts. Players redistribute real structural weight before confronting its authority, preventing a purely symbolic victory that collapses people's homes.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Terrace rams carry balanced loads on broad cliff paths and need rest shelves. A crystal-shell morph is a proposed short-term defensive form with door clearance, turn radius and safe deactivation constraints.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Surveying, masonry, crystal tuning, lift maintenance, cave farming and mineral reclamation support civilian mastery. Rare crystal extraction competes with maintaining aquifers and habitats, giving restraint economic meaning.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Fault exports minerals and structural expertise, importing food diversity, tool steels and communication parts. Royalties can fund common foundations or entrench claim holders; the regional quest determines oversight rather than creating free resources.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Housing uses adaptable terrace modules with inspectable load budgets. Players decorate within safe envelopes; structural failures route through repairable instance states and never allow another player to undermine a neighbor's stored home.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Craft standing values accurate surveys; communal trust values honest warnings and fair relocation. A prestigious builder can lose public authority without losing the player's learned profession, separating social consequence from punitive skill deletion.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kiln makes tools, Rime preserves old surveys and Daybreak arbitrates compensation. Each ally must accept updated measurements; precedent cannot overrule observable danger merely because the founding treaty called the canyon stable.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Escapement cuts high-speed routes through slow review processes, while Impulse demands mass excavation. Reed challenges the Choir's preference for a single authoritative resonance, turning harmony into a debate over whose measurements count.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A terrace-settlement event stabilizes foundations, moves goods and opens relief paths. Choices prioritize different services, but protected core access returns through reconstruction even if the most prestigious hall cannot be saved.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Load puzzles show support paths and movement joints through gauges and tactile-like diagrams. Brute force can worsen a local chamber, but checkpoints and reset braces keep failed experiments bounded and teachable.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Counterweight Halls combine old civic machinery, worker shelters and evidence of manipulated surveys. Several traversal solutions support different first Oaths and a tool-only route, so Earth allegiance is never the entrance key.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A subterranean crystal organism surfaces beneath a trade pass after overmining. Players negotiate alternate freight routes and vent pressure before combat; the organism's habitat is part of the resolution rather than disposable scenery.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Earth can brace marked structures, expose mineral traces and alter authored cover slots. Crystal resonance can carry warnings or interfere with machinery, but every interaction has explicit range, recovery and ownership in systems contracts.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest chooses whether to reinforce the old capital district, relocate it or distribute services across terraces. Each option affects memory, expense and safety while compensating displaced residents through the story.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Pella must decide how to present alarming evidence without overstating uncertain measurements. A cave farmer asks players to protect ventilation from a lucrative lift expansion, making invisible infrastructure emotionally concrete.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Earth plus Sound can improve surveys or enforce a false consensus; Earth plus Gravity changes loads without removing responsibility. Multi-Oath use requires a published structural plan and civilian fallback, not miracle architecture.

## 37. Sound
