## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Protection is legitimate when the protected can question it. Daybreak culture values public truth and mutual care, but radiant certainty can turn disagreement into presumed guilt and make compassion conditional on obedience.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed refuge wards sheltered people after the Fracture and became public courts. The covenant's duty to illuminate danger gradually expanded into a claim to judge hidden motives, an expansion the main story places under scrutiny.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sanctuary councils administer care while civic benches adjudicate disputes with documented appeals. Wardens answer to both, making delay possible but also preventing a single charismatic protector from legally owning every decision.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Helia Mar has defended refugees against powerful enemies and now seeks custody of the fragment for public safety. Their contradiction is offering generous protection while assuming a protected person owes obedience in return.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorin Kes is a plaza medic who witnesses the Archivist's death and refuses to let political investigators delay care. Cleaners, scribes, shelter cooks and petitioners establish the plaza as lived civic space before it becomes an icon.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Lantern Ward is a mixed refuge district outside the monumental center. Residents benefit from sanctuary but challenge identity checks and curfews that make temporary protection feel like permanent supervision.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Aureate Steps is the PROPOSED name for Daybreak's capital, not a recovered name. Its great plaza and the Archivist's death there are LOCKED; the exact stair geometry, districts and urban name await approval.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The central temperate basin links northern passes, western producers and eastern routes. Tributaries from Reed flow toward Sanguine and Escapement, making the capital's prosperity depend on upstream care and downstream access.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Marble arcades, sun-shading colonnades and visible public thresholds frame civic authority. Shelters and clinics share the same durable materials as courts, preventing the architecture from implying that only judgment deserves grandeur.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ivory stone, sun-forged gold accents and lotus motifs are recovered reference traits. Weathered paving and repair scaffolds humanize monuments; reflective surfaces need controlled glare for readability and accessibility.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Prism-backed coursers graze public meadow belts and pull clinic carts. Urban birds and pollinator gardens support ordinary life, while excessive ward brightness can disrupt nocturnal wildlife and neighboring residents.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A moderate basin climate brings seasonal fog, summer heat and flood risk. Magical radiance does not abolish night or weather; ward failures reveal dependence on maintenance rather than proving that residents were morally unworthy.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Public meals, open petitions and care shifts define civic participation. Covenant duty is to offer protection without demanding confession, with institutional disputes over whether that ideal can survive security threats.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Wardens defend evacuation corridors and civilian gathering points, using proportional force and documented custody. Military leaders may demand emergency exceptions, but the narrative preserves consequences for turning sanctuary into coercive occupation.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Light combat emphasizes wards, support, exposure and committed radiant attacks. Revealing an authored concealed target is not omniscient truth detection; source effects cannot decide guilt, intent or narrative reliability by themselves.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sun-forged spears, broad ward shields and ceremonial focus rods have clear protective silhouettes. Lotus or radial ornament stays away from grip and joint motion, preserving practical use and distinct visual hierarchy.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ivory lamella and gold-edged guard plates sit over plain civilian cloth. Medics, judges and frontline wardens have separate silhouettes so holy imagery does not make every public servant a heavy-armored paladin.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers maintain wards and clinical sanctuaries. Their professional obligation distinguishes a reliable observation from a moral conclusion, making false certainty a civic failure even when a spell functions perfectly.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth specialists escort threatened witnesses through service passages and investigate corrupt ward offices. Their legitimacy depends on consent and review; concealed action is not automatically evil merely because Daybreak favors visibility.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include abusive emergency wardens, forged-evidence brokers and failed protective constructs. Genuine protectors remain present in opposing branches, preventing dissent from revealing the entire institution as secretly malicious.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Unblinking Witness is a ward intelligence that mistakes unresolved suspicion for permanent danger. Players protect people flagged by incomplete records while preserving real defenses, then establish who may challenge its future judgments.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Warded road coursers support ambulances and protected caravans. A radiant mantle morph is a proposed temporary rescue form, with readable boundaries and no implication of moral purity, invulnerability or permanent social authority.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Medicine within the fiction, ward maintenance, stonework, public records, food relief and legal advocacy support civilian progression. Players can advance through care and evidence gathering without joining a military order.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Daybreak exports sanctuary services, arbitration and precision light work while importing food and construction materials. Donor dependence can distort protection priorities, so transparent budgets matter as much as luminous ideals.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Housing includes mixed-income courtyard blocks and refuge rooms, with ward sockets and shared kitchens. Sanctuary status protects access during conflict; players cannot place judgment effects that police other players' identities or movement.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Institutional honor measures compliance with civic procedure; public trust measures fair protection. Defying an unlawful order may reduce the former while increasing the latter, and appeals can repair standing without erasing the act.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Rime supplies records, Tidal supports healing infrastructure and Fault maintains safe public buildings. Partners may refuse intrusive inspection without forfeiting friendship, preserving privacy as a legitimate value inside a protection-focused alliance.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Missing Court shelters people who fear public exposure; Bitterglass demands room for uncertainty; Sanguine contests compelled testimony about family obligations. These disputes create genuine safety tradeoffs rather than obvious heresy targets.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A sanctuary-overload event follows a regional disaster and tests shelter, food and triage logistics. Players decide how to distribute limited services transparently, with no ranking of lives by faction standing or moral score.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ward puzzles distinguish observed facts from interpretations using testimony, physical traces and explicit uncertainty. Illumination reveals clues but never replaces reasoning or forces a player to confess an invented belief.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Chamber of Appeals contains contested judgments and a malfunctioning protective network. Players can investigate through records, witness access and spatial encounters regardless of their first Oath, then publish or protect evidence selectively.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A radiant guardian expands a protective perimeter until it traps the people it guards. Public roles dismantle unsafe segments, escort residents and maintain genuine threats outside, demonstrating that more protection is not always better.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Light can mark safe routes, cleanse authored hazards and expose designated traces. It cannot universally invalidate Shadow, read private player data or permanently alter another character's identity under a morality label.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest limits emergency custody through independent appeal, shared sanctuary governance or a narrowly empowered civic bench. Helia's alliance depends on the player's safeguards, not unconditional surrender of the fragment.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorin seeks testimony from frightened bystanders without turning a clinic into an interrogation room. Another story helps a former warden make restitution while respecting victims who do not wish to forgive them.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Light plus Shadow can protect a witness while preserving accountability; Light plus Poison can improve testing or overstate purity. Multi-Oath practice separates visibility, truth and legitimacy rather than collapsing them into one power.
