## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Growth succeeds when different lives can flourish together. Sanctuary culture values mutual care, yet its institutions can confuse interdependence with a right to absorb dissenting people, species or settlements into one managed organism.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed survivors shared seeds and shelter after the Fracture, binding restoration to reciprocal cultivation. Later caretakers made living infrastructure so effective that leaving its network became materially difficult even when legally permitted.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Garden commons elect seasonal stewards, while sanctuary councils regulate grafts affecting shared roots and waterways. Consent records must be renewed when a graft changes function, preventing old permission from becoming unlimited biological ownership.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Yara Moss protects devastated land and believes no one should be abandoned. Their flaw is assuming compulsory integration is preferable to freely chosen risk, especially when isolated families refuse the common root network.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tovin Hale breeds resilient food plants and wants to preserve an ungrafted family variety with lower yield. Care workers, animal handlers and independent growers show that sanctuary membership is a negotiation, not a single pastoral personality.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Dessa Rowan (character-07-handler) runs a small rootpath-hart transport service and negotiates animal refusal, rest and livelihood in two optional quests. Her service uses existing creature concepts; it is not an implemented mount system.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Gleaning Margin is a mixed farm town at the forest edge with open commons and private plots. It tests whether an independent household can receive emergency care without surrendering long-term control over its land.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Rootmeet grows around several connected mature trees with inspectable structural supports and ground-level services. Living bridges have dormant-season backups; sap flow and canopy light place real limits on expansion.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The wet western lowlands receive ash-enriched rivers from Kiln and cold water from Rime. Forested slopes buffer flooding toward Tidal, while overgrowth into Fault access roads reveals that restoration can impose costs on neighbors.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Living timber frames combine trained roots with replaceable woven walls and stone hearth islands. Occupied structures include rot inspections, firebreaks and accessible ground routes rather than assuming every resident climbs a giant tree.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Bark, moss, amber sap, seed husks and weathered stone align with recovered references. New growth has distinct texture from load-bearing old wood; lushness varies with season, shade and management.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Seed-carrier browsers distribute fruit and maintain glades; predators prevent destructive overbrowsing. Rootpath harts are trained animals with welfare and forage needs, not summonable equipment that disappears when inconvenient.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Rain, fungal blooms and seasonal canopy shifts define weather. Drought and invasive overgrowth are different events requiring different responses, so healing the land never means maximizing biomass everywhere.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Shared nurseries, food preservation and animal care organize daily work. Covenant duty is stewardship proportionate to benefit, with nonphysical contributions accepted and the right to refuse personal biological alteration explicitly protected.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Defenders protect food stores, seed diversity and evacuation trails. Living barriers delay invaders but must leave wildlife and civilian corridors; weaponizing disease or forced assimilation is a faction controversy, not routine heroic practice.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Nature combat emphasizes preparation, support and bounded zones of growth. Healing, summons and roots require source-backed behavior and clear counterplay; fertile ground is not a justification for infinite healing or uncontrolled replication.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Pruning hooks, laminated bows and seed-pod implements retain recognizable tool uses. Living weapon components need dormant storage and replaceable grips, with no implied sentience unless a specific approved story establishes it.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Bark-laminate guards and woven fibers adapt to movement without engulfing the wearer. Farmers, wardens and biomancers have distinct work silhouettes; consent markers identify optional grafted equipment.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers diagnose graft compatibility and restore damaged habitats. Their civic code prioritizes informed consent and reversibility where possible, making the temptation to perform an emergency miracle without permission narratively meaningful.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth wardens use scent discipline, soft paths and observation hides. They cannot command all wildlife as an omniscient sensor network; defenders read disturbed leaves, unfamiliar grafts and broken care markers.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include runaway maintenance growth, territorial fauna and coercive caretakers protecting a failing experiment. A living plant network may be hazardous without being morally evil or secretly controlled by one villain.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Unending Orchard is a productive living symbiosis whose roots invade independent farms while feeding households with no immediate substitute. Cutting it back reduces food, moving it consumes another habitat and leaving it intact restricts autonomy. Players can fight dangerous growth, but no hidden malicious component removes the need for a costly settlement.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Rootpath harts travel designated forest routes and require proper stable space. A bark-skin morph is a proposed temporary adaptation with explicit self-consent, readable anatomy and no assumption that the player's identity is permanently absorbed.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Farming, husbandry, seed keeping, graft carpentry, fermentation and habitat monitoring support full civilian progression. Yield, resilience, diversity and labor burden are distinct values rather than one universal harvest score.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Graft exports food, fibers and ecological restoration while importing tools, salt and mineral supplements. Crop diversity protects trade resilience; pushing every farm toward the highest-yield template risks a continent-wide shared vulnerability.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Homes grow through authored modular stages with inspectable supports and optional garden plots. Players choose living or conventional interiors, and inactivity never lets unchecked growth destroy furniture or revoke ownership.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stewardship standing tracks reliable care; autonomy trust tracks respect for boundaries and fair access. Refusing a communal graft can lower institutional favor while improving trust among independent growers without blocking basic services.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tidal manages downstream flow, Bitterglass tests plant medicines and Kiln supplies controlled-burn expertise. These partnerships work only when chemical, fire and water interventions have purpose-specific ecological limits.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Impulse favors standardized crops and mechanized harvest; Sanguine contests inheritance of living estates. The Sanctuary also debates its own expansion, so ecological conflict cannot be reduced to virtuous Nature versus evil industry.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] An overgrowth event asks players to prune escape routes, relocate animals and preserve food-bearing sections. Success creates several viable recovery landscapes; burning everything may be fast but requires costly soil and habitat repair.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Graft puzzles redirect light, moisture and support along visible growth nodes. Growth occurs in bounded authored stages, with reset cuttings and tool solutions for players whose first Oath has no plant magic.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Common Root is an occupied maintenance network beneath the capital, combining care wards, seed vaults and disputed control junctions. The central question is who may disconnect, not whether all shared infrastructure should die.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] An old canopy guardian moves through farmland after its habitat corridor closes. The event restores passage and protects harvests, allowing animal handling, engineering and defense to contribute without requiring a kill.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Nature can stabilize designated soil, cultivate specific plots and reveal ecological traces. Growth never consumes unlimited geometry, blocks all roads or permanently changes another player's body or housing without explicit authority.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest establishes voluntary commons, a tightly audited central network or independently managed groves with shared emergency duties. Every branch must budget care for people who cannot maintain private infrastructure.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tovin's heritage seed story asks whether cultural value justifies lower yield during scarcity. Another follows a sanctuary animal that cannot safely return to the wild, complicating the assumption that release always equals freedom.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Nature plus Poison can heal or produce dependency; Nature plus Blood can support recovery or coerce kinship. The fragment broadens options but never substitutes for consent, ecological monitoring or maintenance labor.

## 37. Sound

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Insects, working water, pruning rhythms and close communal voices vary with season and canopy density. Overgrowth warnings interrupt the sound bed clearly, with readable plant-state animations and captions.

## 38. Visual motifs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Branching supports, graft seams and nested seed shapes emphasize negotiated connection. The emblem shows two roots joined by a removable tie, making consent visible rather than presenting endless consumption as harmony.

## 39. Palette and materials

[RECOVERED observations: REF-107; PROPOSED extensions: SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Recovered bark, moss and amber sap anchor the palette. New linen, clay and fruit colors identify civilian spaces, while threat states change structure and motion rather than simply turning all vegetation red.
