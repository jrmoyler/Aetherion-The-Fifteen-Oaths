## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Imagination lets people rehearse a different life, but an audience deserves to know when it is being asked to believe. Theatre culture prizes invention while disputing whether a beautiful deception can ever substitute for informed choice.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed illusionists sheltered refugees by disguising damaged places after the Fracture. Temporary stages became civic districts, and the power to change appearance grew into political authority over which version of a place counted as public reality.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] District troupes share a civic stage charter, while plain-surface inspectors certify essential routes and contracts. The central conflict is whether those inspectors protect consent or merely enforce the tastes of established patrons.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Mirren Fane is an acclaimed director who uses illusion to keep a divided city functioning. They conceal failing infrastructure behind a comforting civic spectacle and must choose between preserving hope and allowing people to act on truth.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Seli Dorn runs a plain-surface market stall and can spot practical discrepancies others overlook. Stagehands, tailors, guides and accessibility workers reveal the labor behind apparent impossibility.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Plainmarket is a stable trading district where appearance-altering effects are disclosed and optional. It gives visitors a baseline for contracts and navigation without treating artistic participation as foolish or inherently dishonest.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Manydoor grows through rearrangeable facades around a persistent structural street plan. Interior performance spaces can change within authored bounds, while hospitals, exits and public services retain recognizable anchors.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Dry eastern valleys connect Daybreak, Reed, Observatory, the Court, Sanguine and Escapement. Mirages and unusual acoustics inspire illusion craft, but food, drainage and travel still follow physical geography beneath the spectacle.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Reversible panels, mirrored courtyards and stage-like thresholds make transformation mechanically legible. Backstage corridors and structural columns remain readable, giving artists tools instead of making every building arbitrarily impossible.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Recovered navy, ivory, faceted mirrors and gold anchor surfaces. Proposed canvas, chalk and exposed timber show backstage labor; mirrors use controlled reflection so navigation does not depend on disorienting glare.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Mirror-mantled foxes use patterned fur and behavior to evade predators without literally becoming magical holograms. Altered variants require separate concepts, preserving a meaningful distinction between natural camouflage and Oath-driven illusion.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Dry summers, cool nights and episodic valley fog shape the region. Illusions may change perceived weather only in bounded authored scenes; actual hazards retain accessible cues and cannot be hidden by decorative spectacle.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Residents celebrate rehearsal, costume exchange and public disclosure of staged experiences. Covenant duty is to mark consequential illusions and preserve informed exit, while political patrons seek exceptions for supposedly beneficial civic myths.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Forces use decoys, misdirection and rehearsed repositioning to reduce casualties. False surrender, hidden civilian hazards and impersonation of protected services create consequences, keeping deception from becoming an unlimited military excuse.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Arcane combat emphasizes perception, prediction and clearly bounded misdirection. Decoys have learnable tells and targeting rules; illusions cannot falsify accessibility warnings, real purchases, moderation messages or system-level player consent.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Mirror-backed fans, slender focus blades and articulated stage rods distinguish gesture and reach. Reflective facets are mounted safely, with closed transport states and alternate low-glare presentation for combat readability.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Layered navy and ivory garments hide practical light armor beneath gold-edged paneling. Performers and infiltrators share construction techniques but differ in silhouette, avoiding identical glamorous robes for every social role.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers are illusion designers and perceptual safety inspectors. They record what a scene changes and preserve a stable exit reference, making the ethics of immersion a practical profession rather than a vague theme.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth performers use rehearsed impersonation, costume cues and service routes. Their disguises require context-specific verification; defenders can compare behavior and records without a universal anti-illusion spell that trivializes the faction.

## 20. Creatures and enemies
