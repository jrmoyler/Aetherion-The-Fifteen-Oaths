## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Expression is a shared act, yet no chorus owns its singers. Reed culture values attentive listening and improvisation while confronting institutions that mistake a pleasing public harmony for genuine consent.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed cliff communities used synchronized calls to coordinate rescue after the Fracture. A practical warning tradition became an artistic and political covenant, eventually giving licensed conductors excessive authority over public speech.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Amphitheater assemblies pair open speaking sessions with quiet written submissions. Conductors coordinate emergencies but must surrender that authority afterward; prolonged emergencies are the central route by which temporary discipline becomes permanent control.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Lio Senn values public improvisation and has already ceded authority in ordinary performances, but insists that emergency coordination needs a final voice. Their conflict with the player is a defensible limit question: who decides when immediate danger ends, and who accepts responsibility if a plural command fails?

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Amri Fell repairs acoustic panels and communicates primarily through sign and written notation. Their expertise exposes the error of equating hearing, verbal fluency or public performance with belonging to the Resonance.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Hush Vale lies in a sheltered notch where residents cultivate quiet crafts and rest. It supplies counterweights and textiles to the cliffs, while refusing the capital's expectation that every civic gathering be musical.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Cantilever occupies a broad escarpment with windbreak courts and grounded accessible routes. Suspended stages and bridges dramatize exposure, but essential services always have sheltered paths for people unable or unwilling to glide.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Northern east-facing cliffs receive turbulent winds crossing the Storm mountains. Reed connects inland Daybreak, the Theatre valleys and Observatory headlands, turning control of safe crossings into a consequential political resource.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Carved timber ribs, tension cables and adjustable acoustic baffles create open structures that respond to weather. Large occupied buildings anchor to rock, and flexible bridges have visible load limits and calm-weather alternatives.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Pale wood, woven reed, limestone, red cord and aged metal align with recovered reference traits. Tension wear, polished handholds and repaired sailcloth communicate use better than covering the realm in floating ornaments.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sail-feather climbers nest in sheltered cracks and spread cliff plants on their plumage. Glider traffic disturbs breeding paths; seasonal route closures can support husbandry and gathering without making wildlife mere hazards.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Crosswinds, downdrafts and low cloud complicate travel; leeward gardens provide ordinary calm habitat. Wind warnings use moving flags, captions and vibration-compatible cues rather than sound alone.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Communal kitchens use wind-driven machinery while storytellers, signers and scribes share cultural authority. Civic obligation is to help carry essential warnings in an accessible form, never to sing or accept one approved voice.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Scouts secure ridge approaches and maintain evacuation corridors before dueling on exposed stages. Acoustic suppression can protect a rescue but becomes abusive when used against peaceful assembly, a distinction reflected in regional law.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Wind and sound combat favors spacing, readable rhythm and displacement with limits. Environmental cliffs cannot turn ordinary crowd control into unavoidable instant kills; ledge protection and recovery belong in systems validation.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tuning-fork polearms, weighted reed staves and compact bow instruments express adjustable tension. Every musical weapon needs credible grip and impact surfaces; decorative strings cannot replace a mechanically legible blade or projectile origin.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Light articulated cloth, carved wood guards and cord fastening preserve range of motion. Rescue harnesses are visible on gliders and cliff workers; ceremonial flowing sleeves have practical shortened combat variants.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers tune civic warning towers and mediate dangerous resonance. Their tools show frequency and pressure through movement as well as tone, ensuring magical expertise does not exclude residents with hearing differences.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth practitioners use counter-rhythm, cloth-damped gear and backstage routes. They suppress only a bounded local signature; readable disturbed ropes, footprints and pressure vanes keep infiltration from becoming perfect invisibility.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include coercive chorus marshals, reckless glider raiders and displaced nesting animals. Musicians are not a hostile class, and antagonists retain distinct reasons for valuing order, livelihood or artistic freedom.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Perfect Chorus is an ensemble of willing human performers whose protective resonance also suppresses nearby testimony. Members disagree about when to stop because a real crosswind threatens the crowd. The encounter combines stage traversal, protection and negotiation, with a resolution that redistributes risk instead of revealing universal mind control.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Lio Senn (character-06-leader) coordinates the Perfect Chorus encounter. Ensemble members remain willing participants with distinct opinions; defeating an encounter state does not mean killing a culture's artists.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Reedwing gliders launch from inspected platforms and return along wind-marked routes. A windborne morph is a separate proposal requiring landing guarantees, camera comfort options and traversal boundaries rather than unlimited flight.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Instrument making, sail weaving, acoustic surveying, courier gliding, accessible notation and windmill repair support civilian mastery. Performances can earn standing, but equivalent nonperformance contribution paths remain first-class.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Reed exports textiles, forecasting and sound engineering while importing structural fasteners and grain. Cultural commissions can fund shared stages or reward only favored voices; procurement reforms affect who can afford to speak.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Cliff homes cluster behind windbreaks, with optional performance balconies and quiet interior rooms. Housing customization includes sound control; no player can impose disruptive audio on neighboring households or block sheltered public circulation.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Artistic esteem and civic trust remain separate. A flawless official performance may please patrons while silencing witnesses, and a technically rough emergency message can build trust by reaching people in danger.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Storm carries warnings, Graft cultivates reed materials and Daybreak hosts public appeals. Partners disagree constructively about signal accuracy, audience access and when a performance has been turned into political propaganda.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Fault prefers a stable shared resonance; the Theatre profits from persuasive illusion. Reed contests both when they erase minority testimony, while acknowledging that improvisation without safety coordination can endanger crowds.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A crosswind gathering event replaces a canceled festival with a rescue and rebuilding effort. Players direct traffic, secure bridges and preserve displaced performers' livelihoods; music is an optional contribution rather than a rhythm-game gate.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Acoustic puzzles offer waveform shapes, moving membranes and captioned call patterns alongside audio. Solutions route pressure and echo through visible chambers, with adjustable playback and no required pitch recognition.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Muted Amphitheatre contains blocked testimony channels, maintenance galleries and occupied refuge stages. Players reconstruct several viewpoints and restore accessible communication before confronting the authority that ordered the silence.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A great cliff guardian produces dangerous downdrafts while seeking a lost nesting ledge. The public encounter opens a safe migration route and shields settlements, with ground-based roles equal to gliding combat.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Wind turns designated sails, clears bounded fumes and reveals hidden sound paths. Silence or pressure effects cannot globally mute chat, disable accessibility cues or permanently remove another player's ability to participate.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest reforms public speech through rotating conductors, independent stages or a charter limiting emergency powers. Each branch retains warning coordination but distributes cultural influence and accountability differently.

## 35. Side stories
