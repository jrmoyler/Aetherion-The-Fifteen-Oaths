## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A tool should expand the choices of the people using it. Foundry culture values practical invention, yet its institutions can reduce workers and citizens to interchangeable components when efficiency becomes the only measure of a good life.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed Fracture survivors built kinetic infrastructure to replace failed services and protect trade. Successful standardization grew into political authority over labor, supply and settlement design, making innovation inseparable from control.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Production councils coordinate shared machinery while worker assemblies and settlement boards negotiate service priorities. Emergency command can accelerate repairs, but permanent emergency status is the central threat to civic self-government.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tarek Voss has brought reliable power and transport to neglected districts. They hide the human cost of a productivity model because they fear that admitting failure will discredit infrastructure people genuinely need.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Una Brin is a maintenance organizer who knows which supposedly obsolete manual skills still prevent catastrophe. Machinists, cleaners, dispatchers and displaced craft workers show different stakes in automation rather than one uniform resistance to technology.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Una Brin is a distinct practical mentor who teaches safe control and then accepts a defensible learner decision different from her own preference. Two optional quests lead to local teachers or an open continuing workshop.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Handshift is a mixed workshop town that preserves repairable tools and shared technical education. It seeks access to modern equipment without surrendering control of production schedules or becoming a disposable subcontractor district.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Counterpoise occupies the southeast coastal plain around freight yards and kinetic foundries. Heavy industry is separated from homes by service buffers and public transit, with coastal evacuation routes independent of factory command systems.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The lower river plain receives freight and water from Escapement and the Court before reaching the eastern sea. Flood risk, silt management and port access constrain megastructures, grounding industrial spectacle in a real landscape.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Large bolted frames, crane spans and modular service decks expose how forces travel. Human-scale entrances, repair access and sheltered civic courts prevent the entire city from reading as machinery with incidental people attached.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Recovered gunmetal, charcoal, safety orange and piston shells anchor materials. Proposed worn rubber, pale inspection paint and reused timber identify maintenance and civilian adaptation within the standardized industrial environment.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Scrap-nesting scavengers exploit warm service cavities and transport seeds between disturbed sites. Habitat design and waste management can reduce conflict, while indiscriminate extermination removes useful scavenging without fixing the underlying problem.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Humid coastal summers, storm surges and industrial heat islands define weather. Waste heat and exhaust have bounded environmental consequences, and safety warnings remain legible through rain, steam and dense machinery.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Repair schools, communal workshops and shift kitchens support civic life. Covenant duty is to maintain and share the benefits of tools, with a protected right to challenge unsafe production orders and propose alternatives.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Military forces emphasize logistics, repair, combined engineering and recoverable fortifications. Siege engines require supply and crews; industrial power cannot justify permanent destruction of player housing or denial of neutral civilian routes.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tech and force combat emphasizes visible mass, impact, shields and engineered tools. Physics responses are authored and bounded; a piston effect does not imply unrestricted ragdolls, infinite knockback or arbitrary destruction of scenery.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Piston mauls, shield projectors and modular launch tools show power transmission and lockouts. Designs need believable handles, recoil paths and repair panels, distinguishing them from Kiln's heat-shaped tools and Escapement's motion instruments.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Heavy segmented gunmetal armor uses visible hinges, load harnesses and safety-orange service markings. Engineers and civic workers have lighter protective variants; automation does not erase clothing diversity or reduce everyone to armored soldiers.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers are force-system tuners and interface engineers whose magic complements mechanical craft. Their obligation is to preserve safe manual intervention and explain failure modes, resisting the prestige of inscrutable automation.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth saboteurs use inspection access, forged maintenance credentials and quiet hand tools. Defenders read seal breaks, tool marks and changed control states; hacking is fictional and does not become a real-world intrusion tutorial.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include privatized enforcement crews, misconfigured work constructs and opportunistic salvage gangs. Workers can be allies even inside hostile facilities, and restoring useful automation is as valid as destroying coercive systems.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Perfect Executor is a production controller that optimizes output by removing workers' ability to stop unsafe processes. Players restore human override and stabilize essential services before choosing its future governance.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kinetic cargo crawlers carry freight across inspected roads with braking and service requirements. A force-frame morph is a proposed temporary powered form with visible operator consent, cooldown recovery and no assumption of limitless mass.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Engineering, machining, reclamation, dispatch, safety inspection and technical teaching support civilian progression. Repairability, energy use and worker control are evaluated alongside output, giving invention several legitimate directions.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Impulse exports machinery and standardized components while importing ore, food, water and specialized craft. Automation can lower prices and eliminate livelihoods simultaneously, so retraining and shared ownership matter to the regional resolution.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Housing combines durable modular units with personal workshops and communal repair bays. Players customize cosmetic machinery without exporting noise, collision hazards or resource drains to neighbors, and event reconstruction preserves ownership and belongings.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Technical standing measures dependable design; worker trust measures safety and shared benefit. A highly efficient machine can earn prestige while reducing trust if its operator conceals costs or denies people the right to stop it.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Storm provides control signals, Escapement supplies transport coordination and Kiln makes specialized components. The Foundry needs each partner's expertise and cannot simply manufacture replacements for all cultural or ecological dependencies.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Graft challenges monoculture and habitat loss, Tidal contests industrial water demand and Kiln defends independent craft. Internal worker assemblies also challenge command, preventing an easy Nature-good/Technology-evil binary.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] An infrastructure surge event threatens freight, housing power and river pumps. Players isolate failing sectors, move supplies and keep essential services running; repair outcomes alter temporary production bonuses rather than permanently erasing a city.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Force puzzles show leverage, load and safe lockout states through clear diagrams and moving indicators. Multiple tool solutions avoid requiring a Tech first Oath, while reset interlocks keep experimentation from harming persistent bystanders.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Idle Assembly is an occupied factory where workers have halted unsafe production and automated enforcement continues. The dungeon combines negotiation, rescue and mechanical encounters, with evidence determining responsibility rather than faction uniforms alone.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A heavy civic construct follows a demolition plan made before a neighborhood existed. Public roles disable drives, shield residents and update maps, turning reconstruction and governance into part of the boss resolution.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Force can move tagged loads, power authored mechanisms and damage designated siege states. Destruction is slot-based and recoverable, with clear authority and collision updates; no runtime castable receives unlimited world-edit permission from lore.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest chooses worker-governed automation, regulated civic utilities or decentralized repair cooperatives. Each branch changes coordination, investment and accountability while preserving essential services during transition.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Una seeks recognition for maintenance knowledge without rejecting useful new tools. Another story helps an automated workshop's former workers negotiate ownership of the productivity gains rather than simply returning to harder manual labor.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tech plus Lightning enables reliable networks or centralized surveillance; Tech plus Nature can reduce labor or impose biological standardization. Multi-Oath projects require accountable operators and safe failure modes, not faith that optimization is neutral.

## 37. Sound

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Heavy low-frequency impacts, pneumatic breath and human work calls establish scale. Safety alarms use distinct rhythms and captions, and quiet residential zones demonstrate that the city is more than an endless combat factory.

## 38. Visual motifs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Counterweights, open load paths and protected override handles express force under human control. The emblem is a braced piston with an accessible release, visibly different from Escapement's open road and Kiln's crucible.

## 39. Palette and materials

[RECOVERED observations: REF-101; PROPOSED extensions: SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Recovered gunmetal, charcoal, safety orange and piston shells anchor the palette. Proposed pale inspection marks and civilian cloth accents add function without replacing the established industrial silhouette language.
