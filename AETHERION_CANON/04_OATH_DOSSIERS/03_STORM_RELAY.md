## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A message has value only if someone can answer. Relay culture celebrates connection while arguing over whether a reliable network may ever silence destabilizing speech or charge essential communities out of participation.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A proposed chain of emergency storm beacons became a permanent network after the Fracture. Maintenance obligations expanded into political authority because isolated settlements depended on the same towers for warnings and coordination.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tower cooperatives elect delegates to a routing council; public emergency traffic has protected priority. Commercial operators seek exceptions, and local towers can resist central orders at the risk of breaking shared guarantees.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tal Ren coordinates the network and wants universal access, but quietly ranks messages by perceived social importance. Their arc turns on accepting accountable rules that can override their own apparently benevolent judgment.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Oda Kesh repairs remote grounding cables and has repeatedly lost contracts after reporting dangerous shortcuts. Couriers, weather readers and kiosk attendants supply evidence of both the network's benefits and its uneven reliability.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Oda Kesh recurs as a professional rival across an audit commission, a warning-under-uncertainty dispute and a final service-plan review; the relationship can become equal collaboration or respected continuing competition.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Grounding Reach is a pass town with low-tech message houses beside high-energy stations. Its paper couriers remain useful during storms, making fallback infrastructure a lived economy rather than an unexplained game menu.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Crownspan straddles several mountain saddles connected by protected cableways. Public squares sit away from discharge paths; dramatic suspended relay galleries are serviced from stable anchor towers and sheltered evacuation stairs.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Northern mountains connect Rime to Reed and the central Daybreak basin. Pass saddles focus wind and electrical weather, while route alternatives through Fault prevent one seized tower from sealing the continent.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tall open-frame towers lean into cable tension, with ceramic breaks and broad grounding feet. Habitable spaces are low and shielded; dangerous transmission hardware remains visibly distinct from ordinary domestic lighting.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Weathered copper, ceramic insulators, braided conductors and dark wet stone dominate working areas. Cable thickness follows visible load, and replacement splices convey maintenance history without making every building a lightning rod.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Copper-wing kites exploit rising air and roost outside discharge zones. Protective nesting sleeves reduce outages; indiscriminate pest removal harms a species that also warns crews of unstable air currents.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Orographic storms, icing and sudden crosswinds constrain travel. Lightning is channeled along marked corridors, with accessible countdown warnings and grounded shelters; routine outdoor life remains possible between events.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Neighbors share public listening hours and teach verification of relayed information. Civic duty means keeping an addressable response point or staffing a fallback route, with privacy-protecting options for people fleeing persecution.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Relay forces secure continuity, repair teams and message integrity. Their military strength is coordination and rapid repositioning; deliberate civilian blackout is a contested escalation with immediate humanitarian and reputational costs.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Lightning emphasizes commitments that travel through visible relationships or locations. Chained effects need clear target rules and limits; network lore never grants unlimited range, autonomous surveillance or behavior absent from source castables.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Forked conductor staffs, compact line-launchers and grounded blades visibly separate powered and safe states. Coils require credible housings and insulated grips; decorative electrical arcs cannot obscure weapon reach.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Segmented conductive plates sit over insulating cloth, with detachable grounding tails only where safe. Scouts carry flexible climbing equipment, while station guards have broad grounded silhouettes that read differently from Impulse armor.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers tune charge distribution and forecast dangerous buildup. Their staffs include replaceable fuse assemblies, and their professional obligation requires recording outages rather than hiding them to preserve a reliability reputation.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Signal ninjas intercept routes through physical access and coded credentials, using cable ascenders and grounded sheaths. Defender counterplay includes seal logs, route redundancy and observable relay changes rather than omniscient detection.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include contracted interception crews, damaged courier constructs and nests displaced by unsafe tower expansion. A repair solution can bypass combat when hostile behavior stems from failing infrastructure instead of deliberate attack.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Unanswered Circuit is an emergency routing intelligence trapped in retransmitting contradictory evacuation orders. Players isolate harmful loops while preserving essential channels, then decide who can authorize its restored priorities.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Grounded cable runners use inspected anchor lines and have low-speed service access alternatives. A charge-riding morph is only a proposed travel fantasy requiring destination validation, visible arrival and no bypass of encounter or PvP boundaries.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Linework, message authentication, instrument making, weather reading and courier logistics build civilian mastery. Independent couriers remain viable so high-tech convenience does not erase every traditional profession in the region.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Storm sells transmission capacity and weather services, importing insulation and structural repairs. Emergency service is ring-fenced from commercial pricing; factions contest how to fund universal access without embedding covert political control.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Homes use shielded rooftop receivers and shared maintenance stairs. Players customize message rooms and workshops, but no private build can place lethal charge across public paths or harvest other players' communications.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Reliability standing tracks honest maintenance and delivery; civic trust tracks access and privacy. A perfect commercial uptime record achieved by dropping poor districts creates the exact conflict the player must confront.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Reed maintains wind forecasts, Impulse manufactures replaceable components, and Rime preserves authenticated messages. Shared standards benefit each without granting any partner ownership of all traffic or the right to inspect private correspondence.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Missing Court contests permanent message retention; Daybreak wants compulsory reporting of dangerous speech. Kiln and Impulse compete over repair contracts, creating economic disputes distinct from elemental opposition.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A storm-front event divides work among grounding, evacuation and message relay. Players can restore a high-throughput trunk or resilient local meshes; both recover essential warnings but leave different commercial and political outcomes.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Routing puzzles use destination symbols, capacity windows and physical cable paths. Failure trips a local recoverable breaker; captions and spatial markers make the solution available without fast auditory or color-only processing.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Silent Junction contains abandoned routing decks and an occupied repair refuge. Evidence shows a deliberate blackout disguised as storm damage, but identifying its sponsor requires corroboration outside the dungeon.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A roaming charge congregation overloads connected towers across neighboring regions. The event coordinates grounding islands and safe migration of energy; its footprint and rewards cannot incentivize luring lethal weather into civilian hubs.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Charge can restart designated devices, reveal a broken circuit or trigger bounded hazard states. Conductivity is an authored material property, not permission for infinite water-to-everyone damage propagation across a shared world.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest settles who controls emergency routing: a central accountable council, federated towers or licensed operators with enforceable public duties. Each has reliability and capture risks that remain legible later.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Oda's whistleblowing case uncovers both a dangerous contractor and the lost wages of its innocent crews. Another story reunites a family through a paper courier after a perfectly delivered message was misunderstood.
