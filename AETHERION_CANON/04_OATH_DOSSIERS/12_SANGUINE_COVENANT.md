## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A gift matters because it can be refused. Sanguine culture honors mutual sacrifice and remembered care, yet inherited pacts can transform gratitude into ownership when descendants are made responsible for promises they never chose.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed Fracture survivors pooled limited lifeforce to keep isolated settlements alive. Memorial pacts became durable civic institutions, then hardened into hereditary claims that confuse honoring ancestors with controlling their descendants.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kin assemblies oversee communal care while consent tribunals review new and inherited pacts. Leaders disagree whether a descendant may end an obligation whose benefits still support others, making transition funding central to reform.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Adra Kest has personally borne heavy costs to protect their stronghold. They expect others to accept comparable sacrifice and must confront the difference between modeling commitment and coercing people through moral debt.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Oren Sol maintains a public care hall and is bound to an inherited service term they did not choose. Healers, genealogists and unrelated household members show that kinship can be chosen, legal, biological or communal.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Open Hearth is a mixed household settlement that admits people without ancestral credentials. Its success challenges fortress elites while creating practical questions about how newcomers contribute to shared care and defense.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kinhold rises on red-stone terraces above an inland river crossing. Ritual halls sit beside kitchens and recovery wards, making the material work of sustaining sacrifice visible instead of presenting blood symbolism without civilian life.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Southern interior hills connect Bitterglass, Daybreak, Theatre and Escapement. Iron-rich soils and seasonal river flow support grazing and crops; crimson geography is mineral and cultural, not a continent literally soaked in blood.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Thick communal halls, shaded recovery courts and interlinked household thresholds express mutual dependence. Private rooms and withdrawal spaces are equally important, giving architectural form to the right to refuse participation.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Crimson cloth, burnished ivory-gold and glass gems align with recovered references. Red sandstone, clean ceramics and worn wood are proposed everyday materials; ritual spaces avoid gratuitous gore as their sole visual language.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ironbrush herdbeasts graze scrub and support transport, fiber and food economies. Redland packhorns require careful load management and communal pasture agreements, creating husbandry beyond the covenant's combat identity.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Hot dry spells alternate with seasonal floods and cool upland nights. Lifeforce magic does not replace water, nutrition or rest; recovery logistics remain an ordinary civic concern across both prosperity and conflict.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Communal meals, chosen-kin ceremonies and recorded care commitments organize daily life. Covenant duty must specify scope, duration and withdrawal terms; coercive inheritance is an institution to interrogate, not an unavoidable biological destiny.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Forces organize around mutual rescue and disciplined rotation, refusing to treat endurance as an infinite resource. Commanders who demand repeated sacrifice without recovery lose trust even if they win battles.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Blood combat uses explicit risk-reward commitments with readable costs and recovery. The system cannot encourage self-harm outside fiction, and narrative sacrifice never requires the player to accept permanent character damage without informed choice.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Ritual lancets remain distinct from battlefield spears and guarded swords. Blood-channel ornament is symbolic or magical, with safe carrying states and no detailed real-world technique for injury or extraction.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Crimson textiles and burnished pale armor emphasize protection of vulnerable joints and recovery after exertion. Care attendants have recognizable noncombat clothing, while ceremonial armor carries removable pact tokens rather than permanent bodily marks.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers witness voluntary lifeforce agreements and support recovery within the fiction. Their professional obligation requires capacity, informed consent and freedom from retaliation, especially when community survival creates pressure to agree.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth retainers investigate forged kin records and escort people leaving coercive pacts. They cannot assume blood grants perfect identity tracking; counterplay uses documented consent, witnesses and physical evidence.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include pact enforcers, forged-ancestry brokers and abandoned ritual constructs. Families and kin groups are internally diverse; opposing an abusive elder does not make every relative complicit or disposable.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The First Creditor is a living pact champion who voluntarily bears a large share of communal care and challenges unilateral withdrawal from agreements. Some obligations are coercive, others were freely renewed and support dependents. A nonlethal contest or mediated hearing establishes standing, but the final settlement must distinguish them and replace lost care.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Encounter identity: The First Creditor is a situational title for Adra Kest (character-12-leader), not an unregistered second person or a requirement to kill the leader.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Redland packhorns serve caravans and relief teams, with rest and pasture needs. A sanguine mantle morph is a proposed temporary combat expression of a voluntary pact, never forced transformation or permanent body ownership.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Care work, genealogy, pact mediation, textile craft, grazing and food preservation support civilian progression. Non-kin professionals can earn equal civic standing, preventing bloodline status from becoming the only meaningful advancement path.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sanguine exports trained relief crews, textiles and caravan support while importing medicines and tools. Mutual-aid pools provide real security but can become coercive debt; reform must replace their useful functions rather than simply erase records.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Housing includes multi-household courts, private withdrawal rooms and guest rights for chosen kin. Players can join communal homes without granting others control of their character, inventory or ability to leave.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Pact standing measures promises freely undertaken; community trust measures care without coercion. Refusing an inherited duty may reduce elite favor while helping residents imagine alternatives, and reconciliation requires changed terms rather than compelled ritual.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Daybreak supports recovery halls, Graft supplies food and Bitterglass tests remedies. Cooperation includes disagreement over consent and evidence, with each institution able to challenge the others' paternalism.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Court protects those escaping inherited claims, Tidal faces parallel relief debts and Impulse seeks labor predictability. These disputes expose the covenant's political choices without depicting shared sacrifice itself as inherently corrupt.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A relief-muster event responds to flood or siege displacement. Players organize food, rest and voluntary service rotations; success rewards sustainable care, while exhaustion creates recoverable staffing gaps instead of glorified endless sacrifice.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Pact puzzles compare terms, beneficiaries and consent histories using clear records. No blood act is required from the real player, and magical locks always have a witnessed noncombat process for story-critical access.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Inheritance Engine combines ritual chambers with public care infrastructure and disputed records. Its challenge is dismantling coercive collection without abandoning people whose survival has become dependent on the same system.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] An ancestral guardian mistakes changing kinship for betrayal and threatens a mixed settlement. The public encounter gathers living consent, protects residents and revises its mandate rather than proving one bloodline superior in combat.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Blood interactions can activate explicitly consented ritual devices or reveal fictional pact traces. They cannot expose private health data, infer real identity, force other players' sacrifice or bypass ordinary damage and consent protections.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest adopts renewable voluntary pacts, publicly funded care or a mixed kin charter with enforceable exit rights. Each branch changes who contributes and who administers care, but no route abandons dependents as a moral shortcut.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Oren seeks release while insisting that the care hall remain open. Another story follows an adopted household member excluded from memorial rites, asking whether shared life can outweigh inherited definitions of belonging.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Blood plus Nature can support recovery or impose assimilation; Blood plus Time can defer costs without canceling them. The fragment cannot transfer obligations onto uninformed people or make consent permanent after conditions change.

## 37. Sound

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Measured heartlike percussion, communal singing and quiet kitchen activity humanize the region. Combat cost cues are clear and optional in intensity, avoiding constant invasive heartbeat audio or gratuitous injury emphasis.

## 38. Visual motifs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Open-linked rings, removable pact tokens and sheltering paired forms express chosen commitment. The emblem includes a breakable tie, making refusal legible alongside solidarity.
