## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Knowledge becomes ethical through how it is tested, shared and used. Guild culture rejects the idea that a substance is inherently good or evil, while facing the temptation to monetize the very vulnerability its expertise can relieve.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Proposed marsh laboratories treated contaminated water after the Fracture, developing both antidotes and weapons from the same research. A mutual safety guild became powerful enough to control certification, supplies and public understanding of risk.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Laboratory houses hold a guild charter, but resident councils and independent testers can challenge permits. The dramatic conflict is whether affected communities can fund evidence when the accused laboratories own most instruments.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Neris Vale advocates open research while maintaining exclusive antidote licenses to fund safety work. They must confront whether genuine public benefit can justify keeping communities dependent on one company's remedy.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Bram Ilex operates a neighborhood assay booth and is losing suppliers after reporting contamination. Glassworkers, cleaners and wetland keepers reveal who handles chemical risk before celebrated researchers receive credit.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Clearfen is a treatment town where clean channels and contaminated channels are physically separated and labeled. Its workers want economic security without concealing the fact that their health and land bear regional costs.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Vitrial combines sealed greenhouse districts with open civic air corridors. Vent stacks, wash stations and independent sampling kiosks are visible civic infrastructure, preventing the poison realm from becoming an implausibly uninhabitable toxic city.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Managed wetlands lie downstream of Fault and between Graft, Tidal, Daybreak and Sanguine. They can filter some contaminants but not erase unlimited waste; saturation and downstream exposure are meaningful design constraints.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Brass-framed glasshouses, washable masonry and compartmentalized work courts support isolation and observation. Housing and markets remain separated from hazardous process areas, with emergency exits independent of sealed research corridors.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Burgundy fabric, brass and luminous green glass match recovered references; new pale wash tiles indicate sanitary spaces. Cracked vessels and patinated fittings convey risk without covering every civilian surface in poison slime.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Filter-shelled crawlers concentrate minerals and require careful habitat management. Harvesting them for reagents can improve short-term treatment while reducing long-term filtration, giving gatherers ecological choices beyond rarity tiers.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Humid fog, stagnant air and seasonal floods alter exposure routes. Hazard cues name inhalation, contact or ingestion pathways without pretending the fiction is real medical guidance; safe detours remain obvious.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Public assay days, shared washhouses and transparent labeling are civic rituals. Covenant duty is disclosure of known risks and participation in remediation, with protection for whistleblowers rather than compulsory self-experimentation.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Guild forces isolate contaminated areas and disrupt supply lines with controlled tools. Chemical warfare creates lasting political consequences and relief obligations; civilian poisoning is not normalized as a clever low-cost tactic.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Poison combat emphasizes readable buildup, area management and preparation. Effects need clear cleanse and recovery rules from systems design; real-world toxicology is neither a balance model nor a recipe for harmful substances.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sealed applicators, staff-mounted assay vessels and short guarded blades communicate precision. Containers have secure caps and visible safe states, with no detailed real-world chemical weapon construction implied.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Layered protective cloth, glass face shields and brass closures differentiate field researchers from masked enforcers. Respiratory protection silhouettes must not hide all facial acting or make every citizen look like an assassin.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers are assay specialists and antidote formulators whose credibility depends on reproducible observation. Their duty is to report uncertainty and adverse outcomes, a direct challenge to politically convenient claims of perfect safety.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth crews use sealed delivery systems, controlled access and falsified labels. Counterplay follows custody seals, sample logs and ventilation changes; the player's investigative path does not require reproducing a real poison recipe.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Enemies include extortionate antidote brokers, escaped experimental constructs and contaminated fauna needing treatment. Researchers and exposed workers are not interchangeable villains, and cleanup can be a valid encounter resolution.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Patient Still is an automated treatment plant that has begun optimizing demand for its own antidote output. Players stop a harmful feedback loop while preserving essential clean-water capacity and evidence of human oversight failures.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sealed marsh walkers distribute weight across soft ground and carry wash-down equipment. A toxin-filter morph is a proposed temporary form with clear limits, never blanket immunity or a claim that exposure has no consequences.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Alchemy, glassworking, assay certification, wetland care, protective tailoring and waste reclamation support civilian play. Success includes reduced exposure and trustworthy labels, not only producing stronger combat consumables.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Bitterglass exports medicines, reagents and testing services while importing fuel and food. Open formulas reduce monopoly rents but require replacement funding for quality assurance, creating a concrete cost for an apparently easy reform.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Homes use clean courtyards, separate workshop drains and optional certified laboratory modules. Player crafting cannot leak persistent hazards into neighboring plots or turn public sanitation into a griefing mechanic.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Research standing measures reliable methods; community trust measures disclosure and remediation. A profitable discovery may raise the former while lowering the latter if its risks are shifted onto people excluded from decisions.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tidal provides flow data, Graft offers biological research partnerships and Rime preserves trial records. Cooperation requires consent and transparent custody, especially when valuable results emerge from community knowledge.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Daybreak officials sometimes treat uncertainty as guilt; Kiln owners resist costly emissions controls. Guild leaders also clash with their own independent testers, keeping the primary conflict institutional rather than elemental prejudice.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A containment-breach event separates source control, evacuation, sampling and cleanup. The public receives plain-language uncertainty updates, and recovery is staged so a region cannot remain permanently toxic after a failed server event.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Assay puzzles compare fictional markers, vessel histories and controlled observations. Solutions are fully invented and accessible, with shape-coded labels and no requirement for real chemical knowledge or dangerous experimentation.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Sealed Distillatory is a compartmentalized plant with protected worker refuges and hidden dependency contracts. Its evidence reveals who benefited from a preventable shortage, while combat remains spatially readable through haze.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A wetland guardian overloaded by accumulated waste becomes a moving contamination source. Players stabilize habitats and redirect flow before confrontation, and the aftermath creates cleanup work rather than rewarding dumping elsewhere.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Poison interactions can test water, mark contamination or create bounded area denial. Nature and water modify authored states only; no unbounded spread, realistic recipe simulation or permanent denial of civilian hubs is permitted.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest chooses public licensing, cooperative ownership or regulated private research with independent testing. Each has funding and capture risks, and all require safe access to essential remedies during transition.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Bram's assay booth needs credible evidence and a sustainable livelihood, not a heroic accusation alone. Another story follows a cleaner whose practical observations expose a flaw in an acclaimed laboratory model.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Poison plus Light can reveal impurities or produce misleading certainty; Poison plus Water can remediate or spread harm. Multi-Oath users must declare purpose, affected area and cleanup responsibility before high-impact civic intervention.

## 37. Sound

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Soft glass taps, vent pressure and deliberate work calls contrast with marsh insects and market bargaining. Hazard alarms have distinct patterns and captions, with reduced haze options preserving both safety and atmosphere.

## 38. Visual motifs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Compartment grids, offset flasks and closed-loop pipe shapes emphasize containment and accountable circulation. The emblem is a divided vessel with an external witness mark, avoiding a universal skull as faction identity.
