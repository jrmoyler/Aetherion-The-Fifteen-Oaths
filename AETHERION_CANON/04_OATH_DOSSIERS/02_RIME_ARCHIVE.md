## 01. Worldview

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Preservation protects the right of future people to decide, but freezing the present can steal that same right. Archivists disagree over whether releasing dangerous knowledge is a breach or the purpose of keeping it.

## 02. Origins

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] After the Fracture, proposed custodians insulated records and living witnesses against further loss. The Archive's covenant binds access to provenance and care, not an invented claim that all remembered accounts are objectively true.

## 03. Political structure

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Collection councils separate custody, interpretation and public access decisions. Emergency seals expire unless independently renewed; the conflict is who can realistically challenge custodians when challengers lack the sealed evidence.

## 04. Leadership

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sera Pell believes preservation protects future choice and openly accepts the political cost of unpopular seals. Her dispute with the player concerns genuine uncertainty over release: she will support a risky thaw only when independent evidence and the affected person's wishes justify it, not merely after a hidden crime is uncovered.

## 05. NPCs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Nemi Or maintains meltwater gauges and wants their parent's cryostasis consent reviewed. Porters, translators and winter gardeners reveal that a frozen library also depends on food, transport and difficult family decisions.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Tarin Esh (character-02-wayfarer) is a travelling Rime-bound courier-historian whose verified dispatch facts intersect the Archivist's route without granting him secret cosmological knowledge or true-Unbound status.

## 06. Settlements

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Meltgate is a seasonal exchange town where cold storage meets the warmer valley. It translates catalogues into common trade speech and gives non-archivists a place to challenge access decisions.

## 07. Capital

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stillfolio terraces into a sheltered glacial valley on rock foundations. Libraries bridge around ice movement instead of assuming glaciers are permanent bedrock; evacuation stacks can be moved before a shelf becomes unsafe.

## 08. Geography

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] High northern catchments feed the western rivers and connect to Storm passes and Fault tunnels. Ice dams are continental hazards, so water release requires downstream consultation even when Archive law calls the ice its property.

## 09. Architecture

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Insulated stone shells surround blue-white ice vaults, with narrow vestibules and warm public rooms. Outer ice is replaceable environmental architecture; manuscript rooms and occupied structures need credible humidity and thermal separation.

## 10. Landscape materials

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Snow-packed granite, translucent ice, wool felts and dark archival metals carry the region. Condensation, scratches and labeled repairs distinguish a working civic archive from an immaculate crystal fantasy.

## 11. Flora and fauna

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Lichen and sheltered fungi feed rime-antler grazers that browse thaw margins. Seasonal migration signals changing snowpack; predators approach towns when sealed roads interrupt that movement, not because frost creatures are inherently cruel.

## 12. Climate and hazards

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Persistent cold gives way to brief melt pulses, whiteouts and crevasse shifts. Slowing zones are covenant anomalies with marked boundaries, separate from ordinary hypothermia or the Time Oath's causality questions.

## 13. Civilian life

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Households share warm reading rooms, preserved food and copying work. Civic duty is care for an entrusted record or vulnerable person; people may serve through translation or cooking without possessing elite magical literacy.

## 14. Military doctrine

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Defense delays attackers and protects evacuation of people and irreplaceable records. Guards prioritize containment with reviewable release conditions; indefinite imprisonment remains a disputed abuse rather than the default reward for institutional loyalty.

## 15. Combat philosophy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Frost favors timing, space denial and protection windows, with explicit recovery from control. Frozen appearance never silently implies a permanent stun, and source castable timing remains governed by audited runtime evidence.

## 16. Weapons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Narrow ice chisels, warding staves and insulated crossbow stocks use practical gloves and grips. Ceremonial blades include provenance tabs; a weapon can communicate custody without needing impossible transparent structural joints.

## 17. Armor

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Quilted layers and overlapping frostproof lamella preserve insulation at the knees and neck. Custodian helmets use clear breathing paths and removable face protection, with distinct rescuer silhouettes in low visibility.

## 18. Sorcerers

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Sorcerers survey ice stresses and preserve unstable materials. They carry split thermometer staffs and archive seals; magically arresting decay creates a future duty to disclose release conditions and ongoing maintenance needs.

## 19. Ninjas and stealth

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Stealth specialists are vault auditors trained in muffled movement and catalog discrepancies. Their equipment favors felt, mirrors and removable seal copies; detection puzzles depend on footprints and documented permissions, not unreadable invisible traps.

## 20. Creatures and enemies

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Abandoned containment constructs and guardians following expired instructions provide enemies. Cryostasis patients are never an anonymous monster category; if transformed, their consent, treatment and personhood remain narrative facts.

## 21. Bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Unthawing Custodian is a living senior archivist defending a patient who validly refused awakening, while an evacuation crisis makes continued stasis dangerous. A duel can secure time and access, but the resolution must preserve consent and fund a difficult transfer rather than expose a conveniently invalid order.

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Encounter identity: The Unthawing Custodian is a situational title for Sera Pell (character-02-leader), not an unregistered second person or a requirement to kill the leader.

## 22. Mounts and morphs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] An insulated archive sled moves cargo and passengers across marked snow roads. A rime-shell morph can be explored as a temporary expedition tool, but cannot replace consent for entering sealed collections.

## 23. Professions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Conservation, translation, cold storage, ice surveying, winter cultivation and assisted care support play outside combat. Copy quality and discoverable provenance matter more than collecting increasingly rare forbidden books.

## 24. Economy

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Rime exports preservation and reliable historical copies while importing food, insulation and heating tools. Paid premium access funds conservation but risks making public memory a luxury, which civic contracts can address.

## 25. Housing

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Homes have warm inhabited cores and optional cold storage annexes. Housing progression expands workshops and shared reading access; players are not asked to maintain a fictional medical cryostasis system through punitive daily login fees.

## 26. Reputation

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Scholarly standing measures honest citation and return of borrowed material; public trust measures fair release. Opening a forbidden vault may lower custody standing while earning survivors' trust, and neither score decides truth automatically.

## 27. Alliances

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Storm carries catalogue requests; Fault stabilizes vault foundations; Daybreak verifies public testimony. Cooperation is strongest when each institution can audit the others instead of treating a common archive as ownership of history.

## 28. Rivalries

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Kiln seeks emergency thaw water, Escapement questions preserved moments, and the Missing Court asks to protect vulnerable identities. These conflicts distinguish necessary privacy from suppression and preservation from perpetual control.

## 29. Public events

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A thaw-front event rescues residents and moves collections before an ice shelf breaks. Choosing which copies travel first changes available research services temporarily; originals have recovery quests rather than undocumented permanent deletion.

## 30. Puzzles

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Catalog puzzles cross-reference witness versions, melt dates and physical wear. Multiple accessible evidence channels resolve them; a player need not memorize dense lore or use a Frost castable to progress.

## 31. Dungeons

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The Uncatalogued Vault stores material excluded from the public index, including conflicting Fracture testimony. It is an investigative dungeon with transport and containment encounters, not a room containing a single unquestionable exposition tablet.

## 32. World bosses

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] A glacial guardian follows an old migratory corridor through settled land during thaw. The public objective opens a safe passage and stabilizes ice bridges; combat is one component of a larger relocation effort.

## 33. Environment interactions

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Frost preserves samples, braces marked crossings and slows selected mechanisms. Thaw is a communicated recovery path; freezing a navigable river cannot deny all users access or rewrite the continent's watershed indefinitely.

## 34. Core quests

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] The regional quest chooses between controlled release, a publicly audited seal or distributed copies of dangerous records. All routes preserve a dissenting witness and carry a different exposure, trust or custody cost into the finale.

## 35. Side stories

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Nemi's parent may have valid reasons to remain in stasis; the player investigates consent rather than deciding that awakening is inherently liberation. A village school requests censored agricultural records for an ordinary food problem.

## 36. Multi-Oath conflict

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Frost preservation can support Nature seed banks or suppress seasonal change; Time acceleration can aid conservation work or destroy provenance. Shared service contracts identify whose consent and records must survive a multi-Oath intervention.

## 37. Sound

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Snow absorption, long breathing spaces and the creak of loaded ice form the sound bed. Warm reading rooms introduce close human voices, while hazard tones avoid being indistinguishable from decorative chimes.

## 38. Visual motifs

[PROPOSED; SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Nested index tabs, interrupted rectangles and sled-like horizontal massing suggest custody and movement. The emblem is an open bracket around a snow line, distinct from generic six-point snowflake branding.

## 39. Palette and materials

[RECOVERED observations: REF-112; PROPOSED extensions: SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL] Recovered overview traits are white, ash tones and translucent ice. Proposed indigo archival textiles and dark rock are secondary civic accents requiring review, not replacement faction colors; warm occupied rooms remain visually distinct from cold storage.
