# World geography

[LOCKED; SRC-USER-DIRECTIVE] Aetherion is one seamless interconnected continent with exactly fifteen major realms. The following relative layout, place names, drainage basins and trade roads are PROPOSED under SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL. They are not final coordinates, measured travel times or a claim that a terrain implementation exists.

## Continental logic

[PROPOSED] A high northern spine runs through Rime and Storm, with the Fault divide extending southwest through the continent's central-west interior. Moist prevailing winds arrive from the western sea: exposed western slopes receive rain, northern elevations retain snow and sheltered eastern valleys are drier. Latitude and elevation both matter; Frost's climate is not created solely by its faction palette.

[PROPOSED] The northern sequence is Rime, Storm, Reed and the Observatory headland. The central band runs Kiln, Fault, Daybreak, Theatre and the Court. The southern interior runs Graft, Bitterglass, Sanguine, Escapement and Impulse. Tidal occupies the connected southwest estuary below Graft and Bitterglass. These are relative bands over irregular territorial footprints, not a grid of equal elemental tiles.

[PROPOSED] The western and eastern coasts belong to the same continental landmass. Reef neighborhoods remain connected to mainland civic services; floating platforms have stable mainland anchors; phasing courts retain persistent public approaches. Small decorative rocks or offshore scenery may exist, but no major realm or essential story route is an isolated island.

## Elevation, water and climate

[PROPOSED] Four conceptual drainage networks make downstream responsibility inspectable. Thawcourse runs Rime → Kiln → Graft → Tidal. Choirwater runs Storm → Fault → Bitterglass → Tidal. Dawnrun runs Reed → Daybreak → Sanguine → Escapement → Impulse. Veilwater runs Observatory → Theatre → Court → Impulse. Every consecutive pair shares a land edge in WORLD_GRAPH.json. Each sequence descends conceptually from headwater to outlet; final terrain must validate actual gradients and avoid rivers crossing watershed divides without a designed pass or canal.

[PROPOSED] Rivers can form local borders and receive minor tributaries from nearby slopes, but the registry does not imply that every point in a realm drains to one channel. Karst losses in the Court require mapped reemergence or groundwater explanation before level lock. Coastal salinity does not travel upstream without tide, surge or pumping conditions. Water conjuration and visual effects do not replace the geographic water budget.

[PROPOSED] Seasonal variation changes snowmelt, soil saturation, river crossings, forage and maintenance. Regional Oaths modulate authored local conditions; they do not eliminate ordinary atmospheric circulation. Storm intensifies charge corridors, Reed shapes exposed airflow, Graft influences canopy and soil, and Kiln adds heat and ash. These influences overlap at borders and remain bounded by systems ownership and recovery rules.

## Transitions rather than biome walls

[PROPOSED] Rime–Kiln transitions through glacial rock, melt channels and increasingly warm volcanic foothills. Kiln–Graft shows ash-enriched farms, managed firebreaks and wet forest. Fault–Bitterglass shifts from canyon drainage to terraced treatment beds and marsh. Reed–Theatre moves from exposed cliff gardens to sheltered dry valleys. Court–Impulse follows damp karst channels toward silt plains and freight infrastructure. Escapement–Impulse changes from road settlements and pasture to humid coastal industry.

[PROPOSED] Border settlements mix materials and professions while retaining distinct civic jurisdictions. Transition kits need shared geology and vegetation plus Oath-specific infrastructure. A border is not a hard color gradient or an invisible elemental damage line. Buffer lands include pasture commons, watershed reserves and relief corridors governed by adjacent realms, never an unannounced sixteenth state.

## Trade and contested access

[PROPOSED] Every adjacency has a baseline walking and pack route in TRADE_ROUTES.json. Fast travel, cableways, gliders, vehicles and ferries add options; none is the sole entrance to a realm. Every proposed land edge has a computed alternate regional path. This topological resilience is a design check, not a measured claim that all detours are equally short or safe.

[PROPOSED] Strategic chokepoints include the Storm–Reed saddle, Fault–Daybreak pass, Bitterglass–Tidal channels and Escapement–Impulse freight crossing. They affect throughput, tariffs, event objectives and military timing. Protected local bypasses and longer regional alternatives prevent one siege or weather event from deleting civilian access or first-Oath choice.

[PROPOSED] Neutral means a protected service charter within an existing realm. The Common Waystation in Daybreak territory supports relief and exchange without becoming a new Oath. The Shared Saddle is a contested Storm–Reed border site whose control affects limited freight policy. The Confluence Chamber is an endgame assembly instance inside region-09, not a detached cosmic island or extra realm.

## Visibility and traversal

[PROPOSED] Landmark sightlines communicate regional identity before arrival: chimney banks, archive terraces, relay spans, open channels, load arches, cliff stages, canopy supports, vented glasshouses, public colonnades, balanced observatories, screened courts, kin halls, reversible facades, linear roads and heavy crane frames. Weather can shorten views but never erase critical navigation or accessible hazard cues.

[PROPOSED] Vertical movement has grounded or mechanically accessible alternatives at essential sites. Gliding, swimming, phasing and gravity traversal can offer optional routes after validation, not prerequisites for understanding the main story. Mount dimensions, grades, turning radii and streaming distances remain production questions until measured in a real level.

## Final-terrain acceptance gates

[PROPOSED] Before approving a world mesh: verify connected land and reachable safe service anchors; terrain-derived downhill drainage; plausible rain shadow and snow line; slope-safe roads and alternate routes; flood and surge refuge; coherent geology across borders; readable silhouette transitions; streaming and navigation limits; and preservation of housing and essential access during event states.
