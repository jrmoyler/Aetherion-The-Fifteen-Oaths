# Distinct creature and boss design supplement

All designs are PROPOSED. Sources: SRC-ASSET-PROPOSAL and SRC-WORLD-PROPOSAL. Human, ensemble, ecological and construct encounters retain distinct ontology and costs. A boss is an encounter role, not automatically a monster or malfunctioning machine.

## 01. The Furnace Witness

Natural fauna: slag-backed burrower. Low six-limbed burrower; broad digging forelimbs, layered mineral back and vent-safe nostrils.
Diet: Sulfur-tolerant lichen and buried root tubers. Niche: Breaks cooled slag crust so pioneer plants can root; warm burrows shelter small animals.
Movement and route: Alternating six-foot crawl; foreclaw excavation; belly kept clear of hot seams. Moves between cooled ash terraces after rainfall; avoids active vent fields.

Boss ontology: guardian, boss.
Four-legged furnace crab with a refractory abdomen, two slag-rake arms and six shuttered chimney plates.
Heavy alternating tripod brace; rotates on planted feet before a rake sweep.
Contains caldera pressure by consuming collected slag and routing heat through maintained vents.

Phase 1: Entry pressure gauge locks. Action: Shutters close in sequence while floor channels heat from the outside inward. Counterplay: Open the visibly cool bypass channel and stand on raised refractory islands.

Phase 2: Two shutters have safely vented. Action: The body braces and rakes slag into a sweeping crescent before a forward furnace release. Counterplay: Move behind the braced leg pair; clear the rake obstruction rather than attack the hottest plate.

Phase 3: Main chimney pressure reaches warning band. Action: The crab exposes its underside regulator during three uneven vent pulses. Counterplay: Interrupt the regulator only during the clear low-pressure interval; failed interrupts add recoverable ash cover.

Resolution: Recalibrate the regulator or disable the chassis; release pressure into an empty diversion basin and reopen the forge route.

Mount: kiln-strider chassis. Four-legged ceramic-shielded walking chassis with rider cradle and vent exclusion zones. Ground walking; heat-safe authored routes only.

## 02. The Unthawing Custodian

Natural fauna: rime-antler grazer. Four-legged grazer with broad padded feet, insulating coat and branching antlers.
Diet: Wind-blown grass, moss and exposed bark. Niche: Keeps snow meadows open; antler-shed minerals return to thaw pools.
Movement and route: Broad-hoofed walk and short bounding trot; lowers head to sweep snow. Descends to wind-scoured feeding slopes in deep snow; returns above thaw streams in spring.

Boss ontology: character, boss, guardian.
Living senior archivist of ordinary humanoid proportions, wearing layered insulation and a removable cryostasis ward frame; hands and face remain readable, with no robotic or monstrous body assumed.
Measured staff-supported steps and protective turns around patient chambers; fatigue changes stance rather than creating a machine breakdown.
Defends a cryostasis patient’s valid refusal to be awakened; ongoing cold storage competes with other archive needs.

Phase 1: The party requests access to the patient. Action: The archivist closes one ward aisle and offers a bounded evidence hearing while maintaining patient protection. Counterplay: Present verified consent records or find the permitted maintenance aisle; attacking wards reduces trust without invalidating refusal.

Phase 2: Archive power must be redistributed. Action: Cold wards move between occupied chambers as attendants protect vulnerable records. Counterplay: Coordinate service relays with attendants, preserving each occupied chamber; optional nonlethal staff exchanges have explicit yield windows.

Phase 3: The patient’s refusal is authenticated. Action: The archivist offers continued costly preservation or a limited alternative evidence route. Counterplay: Accept the refusal and fund a bounded in-world resource solution, seek other testimony, or contest archive policy without coercing the patient.

Resolution: Leave the patient’s valid refusal intact. Continued preservation costs archive capacity; an alternative evidence route costs time or access, and a negotiated reform changes future process without retroactive forced awakening.

Mount: insulated archive sled. Insulated two-runner sled, tow/control assembly and covered cargo or rider cradle. Snow/ice ground traversal; towing method requires design approval.

## 03. The Unanswered Circuit

Natural fauna: copper-wing kite. Light avian body, broad segmented wings and grasping cliff talons.
Diet: Cliff insects and wind-borne seeds. Niche: Controls insects around relay towers and transports seeds between inaccessible ledges.
Movement and route: Thermal soaring, rapid wing tacks and talon-assisted ledge hops. Follows sheltered leeward cliffs during storms; never nests on live relay contacts.

Boss ontology: guardian, boss.
Suspended manta-shaped relay chassis with four anchor cables, six probe fins and a copper spine of ceramic-separated nodes.
Winches between ceiling anchors; grounded service feet deploy before high-load discharge.
Balances communication load between damaged towers; power comes from isolated relay feeds.

Phase 1: Two nodes disagree on route. Action: Probe fins connect a visible branching circuit across arena posts. Counterplay: Choose disconnected islands by tracing the cable lines; ground one marked post.

Phase 2: Half the routing nodes are stabilized. Action: The chassis descends and sweeps its broad fin as charge travels behind it. Counterplay: Cross behind the moving discharge front or open a ground shunt during fin recovery.

Phase 3: The final feed attempts a full-network broadcast. Action: Three nodes count down in different pulse rhythms. Counterplay: Match each tactile/visual node pattern to a shunt, then interrupt the central spine at zero load.

Resolution: Rebuild a limited network route or isolate the damaged relay; communication returns through a redundant service path.

Mount: grounded cable runner. Low cable carriage with insulated handholds and grounding shoe. Authored cable routes; controlled junction transfer.

## 04. The Debt Leviathan

Natural fauna: reedback estuary grazer. Four-legged estuary browser with splayed hooves, reedlike dorsal hair and broad muzzle.
Diet: Salt-marsh reeds, eelgrass and floating seed mats. Niche: Crops channels without stripping banks; hoof depressions form shallow nursery pools.
Movement and route: Splayed-hoof wade, slow swim and side-step on soft banks. Moves with tidal exposure between upper marsh and estuary shoals; inland refuge during surge events.

Boss ontology: natural, Oath-altered, boss, guardian.
Living long-bodied estuary leviathan with six paddle fins, a broad sensory snout and ridged reeflike plates sheltering attached nursery organisms.
Slow tidal swimming and bank-braced turns; a warning breach precedes any defensive sweep near its nursery.
Its seasonal nursery migration protects juvenile reef life but blocks a port channel needed for food shipments.

Phase 1: The nursery enters the channel. Action: The adult circles attached juveniles and raises a directional wake when vessels approach. Counterplay: Read fin and current markers; escort ships through a slower side channel without touching the nursery.

Phase 2: A tide change narrows available routes. Action: The guardian pushes sediment toward a threatened spawning shelf while cargo schedules tighten. Counterplay: Open a temporary safe route using agreed dredge limits or stagger food arrivals; reckless clearance damages habitat.

Phase 3: The final migration window arrives. Action: The adult commits to one reef route and defends the trailing juveniles. Counterplay: Guide the nursery toward a prepared refuge or negotiate a shipping pause, accepting the corresponding habitat or supply cost.

Resolution: Migration and port supply cannot both remain unchanged. A pause costs time/food reserves, a carefully limited diversion costs habitat work, and force carries ecological/reputation consequences; preserve a functioning recovery route in every branch.

Mount: tidal skimmer. Shallow-draft curved-hull skimmer with stable passenger stance. Approved waterways; shore transfer at marked ramps.

## 05. The Buried Adjudicator

Natural fauna: resonance mole. Compact fossorial mammal, oversized digging forepaws and vibration-sensitive jaw.
Diet: Root bulbs, burrowing insects and mineral-rich clay. Niche: Aerates terrace soils and exposes safe small mineral seams without destabilizing load-bearing strata.
Movement and route: Powerful forepaw digging, low-body crawl and short reverse shuffle. Tracks moist strata below terraced farms; avoids engineered foundations and loud quarry faces.

Boss ontology: guardian, boss.
Blind colossal mole with stacked stone shoulder shields, four excavating limbs and a tuning-fork mineral jaw.
Digs shallow authored tunnels; emerges with forelimbs planted before rotating its bulk.
Stabilizes canyon loads by compacting failing strata; resonance core uses surveyed crystal anchors.

Phase 1: The first anchor is struck. Action: The jaw sends a visible ground-ripple survey across the terrace. Counterplay: Stand on stable marked ribs rather than loose infill; use grounded survey posts.

Phase 2: Two anchors are recalibrated. Action: It excavates under one quadrant, then raises a broad slab rather than random terrain. Counterplay: Follow dust vents to the rising quadrant and leave before the foreclaws emerge.

Phase 3: The shoulder plates resonate together. Action: The creature braces for a canyon-wide compacting stamp. Counterplay: Detune one exposed fork while keeping the surviving support anchors intact.

Resolution: Restore the support pattern or relocate the guardian to a prepared quarry; repair all authored terrace breaches.

Mount: terrace ram. Broad-hoofed ram with load-bearing saddle over protected spine. Stepped ground routes; bounded climb angle.

## 06. The Perfect Chorus

Natural fauna: sail-feather climber. Light climber with grasping limbs, feathered membrane surfaces and balancing tail.
Diet: Cliff berries, insects and grass seeds. Niche: Disperses plants onto exposed ledges and nests in abandoned acoustic cavities.
Movement and route: Grasping-foot climb, membrane-assisted leap and feather-braked descent. Follows seasonal updraft shelves; descends into quiet ravines during dangerous gusts.

Boss ontology: character, ensemble, boss.
An ensemble of consenting human performers with distinct reed instruments, standing around a visible resonance stage; separate character bodies and stage hardware remain separate asset components.
Individual breath, hand and foot timing; performers cross between marked stage positions rather than forming one creature body.
Performers disagree over a restrictive public-safety arrangement versus open expression; each retains agency and may alter the ensemble alignment.

Phase 1: A contested public performance begins. Action: Different performers sustain overlapping pressure patterns from marked stage positions. Counterplay: Read rhythm and floor shapes, speak to a named performer during their rest, and preserve a safe audience corridor.

Phase 2: The ensemble splits over the safety order. Action: One group protects the crowd while another continues the disputed refrain, producing moving acoustic gaps. Counterplay: Coordinate a temporary counter-rhythm with willing performers; optional sparring follows explicit consent and yield.

Phase 3: The final shared cadence approaches. Action: The remaining participants choose whether to join a revised arrangement or walk off stage. Counterplay: Offer a verifiable safety plan, accept a reduced performance, or support disruption with its public costs.

Resolution: A negotiated arrangement preserves some expression and limits some risk; a canceled performance preserves immediate safety but damages trust; an unrestricted choice creates a bounded recovery event. No performer is freed by destroying a mind-control device because none exists.

Mount: reedwing glider. Reed-frame membrane wing with body harness and articulated control spars. Glide from legal launch; no unlimited flight or blocked-region bypass.

## 07. The Unending Orchard

Natural fauna: seed-carrier browser. Long-necked browsing quadruped with seed-catching coat and broad grinding teeth.
Diet: Young leaves, fruit and seed husks. Niche: Carries burrs between managed groves; browsing opens germination patches and returns nutrients.
Movement and route: Slow long-necked walk, branch duck and careful root step. Rotates between harvest groves under husbandry schedules; avoids newly grafted saplings.

Boss ontology: natural, Oath-altered, boss, symbiosis.
Living productive root symbiosis spanning several fruit-bearing trunks, mobile root tendrils and linked seed chambers; its distributed organism has no single evil heart.
Very slow redistribution of root load between soil beds; tendrils retract from dry paths and extend toward measured water/nutrient gradients.
Feeds settlements through a cultivated symbiosis whose invasive roots now threaten neighboring habitat and infrastructure.

Phase 1: Roots reach a public route. Action: Nutrient-seeking tendrils lift authored paving while fruiting trunks continue feeding harvest stations. Counterplay: Redirect water into a prepared bed and maintain a harvest path; indiscriminate burning reduces the food supply.

Phase 2: The first growth channel is diverted. Action: Growth shifts toward a different habitat and exposes the cost of merely moving the problem. Counterplay: Survey the alternate soil bed and agree a bounded carrying area before opening the next channel.

Phase 3: The season’s fruit set depends on remaining roots. Action: The colony concentrates growth into whichever connection survives. Counterplay: Choose limited pruning with lower yield, a costly managed extension, or relocation work with explicit habitat mitigation.

Resolution: The organism remains a living participant. Halting invasive roots reduces needed food; rerouting growth consumes other habitat and labor. Record the selected ecological and supply costs rather than presenting “kill the heart” as a universal solution.

Mount: rootpath hart. Root-pattern antlered hart with living padded saddle surfaces. Forest trails; authored root crossings.

## 08. The Patient Still

Natural fauna: filter-shelled crawler. Low many-legged wetland crawler with porous filter shell and protected breathing channels.
Diet: Algae, suspended organic matter and decaying reed fibers. Niche: Filters shallow pools; stores contaminants in shed shell layers that require safe collection.
Movement and route: Many-legged creeping over mud; shell seals during sudden chemical change. Tracks clean-water margins upstream of treatment outlets; retreats to sealed dry hollows during spills.

Boss ontology: guardian, boss.
Massive salamander-shaped distillation construct with six stilts, glass condenser ribs and a coiled tail siphon.
Careful stilt walk above treatment channels; lowers its belly only to seal a reaction basin.
Separates useful compounds from marsh waste through finite filters and heat exchange.

Phase 1: A feed valve is opened. Action: Three reagent streams fill differently textured catch basins. Counterplay: Read labels, bubbles and shape cues; route compatible streams to the correct filters.

Phase 2: Two filters saturate. Action: The construct exhales a directional vapor plume and walks to a clean basin. Counterplay: Close the indicated intake or stand behind the active scrubber; replace one spent filter during recovery.

Phase 3: The central still loses pressure control. Action: Tail siphon draws every pool toward the condenser and exposes the service manifold. Counterplay: Isolate contaminated pools first, then open the safe vent without mixing untreated reservoirs.

Resolution: Stabilize or drain the still through containment tanks; restore water access only after explicit treatment-state validation.

Mount: sealed marsh walker. Sealed stilted walker with enclosed foot mechanism and rider splash screen. Marsh ground routes; stable substrate checks.

## 10. The Unmoored Atlas

Natural fauna: ballast-wing roostling. Small winged roost animal with controllable tail ballast and grasping hind feet.
Diet: High-altitude insects and mineral-coated cliff seeds. Niche: Connects isolated ledge food webs and deposits ballast-grit in nesting basins.
Movement and route: Alternates wingbeats and controlled ballast shifts; lands with a grasping tail. Moves between gravity-stable roost shelves after local anomalies; stranded groups require guided return.

Boss ontology: natural, Oath-altered, boss, colonial.
Living gravity-adapted colonial organism composed of three large buoyant sacs linked by flexible membranes, many small ballast nodules and trailing anchoring filaments.
Transfers dense nodules through its membranes to drift between gravity layers; filaments anchor temporarily to natural cliff holds.
A seasonal colonial migration changes local gravity gradients; observatory models misclassify the movement as a coordinated attack.

Phase 1: The colony enters instrument range. Action: Ballast transfer tilts loose objects toward a moving but traceable gravity center. Counterplay: Anchor at fixed rails and observe the repeating nodule flow; refrain from striking nursery membranes.

Phase 2: Defensive equipment obstructs its route. Action: The organism bunches its sacs and creates stronger gradients while searching for a passage. Counterplay: Disable or reposition a bounded obstruction with local agreement, or guide a diversion using calibrated weights.

Phase 3: The colony reaches its departure gradient. Action: Its membranes stretch across the route between a natural refuge and a strategic platform. Counterplay: Choose the natural migration corridor, accept temporary platform closure, or create a risk-assessed alternate passage.

Resolution: Correct the mistaken attack model and choose whose infrastructure or habitat bears the migration cost. The colony leaves on its own lifecycle; no processor, command core or malicious intent is invented.

## 12. The First Creditor

Natural fauna: ironbrush herdbeast. Heavy grazing quadruped with brushlike mane, strong neck and horned muzzle.
Diet: Coarse iron-rich grass, scrub leaves and seasonal fruit. Niche: Maintains firebreak-like grazing strips; transports seeds through dung and long brush mane.
Movement and route: Heavy four-beat walk, forceful shoulder turn and short herd-defense surge. Shifts between communal wells and seasonal grazing strips; calving routes remain protected.

Boss ontology: character, boss, champion.
Living pact champion of ordinary humanoid anatomy wearing oxblood ritual armor, a visible reserve harness and detachable braided pact cords; no robot, undead or vampire body is assumed.
Controlled martial footwork, tether bracing and visibly tiring recovery; the champion may voluntarily yield or continue within agreed limits.
Voluntarily bears communal pact costs while disputing exit liabilities; neither consent nor the surrounding social pressure should be erased from the encounter.

Phase 1: The communal ledger is challenged. Action: The champion demonstrates the actual burden through marked pact anchors and invites a bounded contest or hearing. Counterplay: Inspect the ledger and obtain consent from affected anchor holders; optional sparring allows nonlethal yield.

Phase 2: Some participants request release. Action: The champion redistributes existing obligations and loses access to the released anchors. Counterplay: Offer substitute contributions or a staged exit; prevent a tether sweep through positioning rather than forcing someone else to pay.

Phase 3: The champion’s own reserve reaches its agreed limit. Action: They must decide whether to continue voluntarily while the community faces a shortfall. Counterplay: Honor the limit, negotiate collective restructuring or accept a temporary service reduction; do not coerce personal sacrifice.

Resolution: The champion can survive and remain a principled opponent or ally. Exits may leave real shared costs requiring negotiation. No solution is “turn off the robot,” and no party member is automatically charged health or resources without the approved game rules.

## 13. The Audience of One

Natural fauna: mirror-mantled fox. Light foxlike omnivore with layered reflective coat and articulated ears.
Diet: Small rodents, beetles and fallen theatre-garden fruit. Niche: Suppresses pests around cultivated mirror groves; reflective coat also displays social signals.
Movement and route: Light quadruped trot, crouched stalk and quick lateral feint. Uses real service passages behind rearranged scenery; shelter access cannot depend on a lethal illusion.

Boss ontology: character, boss, illusionist.
Living illusionist patron in asymmetrical folded garments, accompanied by separate mirror screens and a removable audience mask; the person is distinct from their stage projections.
Deliberate actor-like blocking, hand-directed screen changes and controlled evasive steps; true-body footfalls remain readable.
Defends a curated communal illusion that some residents knowingly prefer while others need access to inconvenient truths.

Phase 1: The patron opens a private performance. Action: Screen images offer conflicting explanations while the true patron moves through marked backstage routes. Counterplay: Track real footsteps and question a willing audience member; close a screen without trapping spectators.

Phase 2: Evidence contradicts the chosen story. Action: The patron divides the stage into competing presentations and invites the party to choose what to reveal. Counterplay: Recover source evidence from real props, preserve a safe exit and distinguish consent to fantasy from deception.

Phase 3: The final audience vote begins. Action: The patron offers a bounded consensual version of the illusion or a public unmasking. Counterplay: Disclose the consequential facts, then allow informed characters to choose; optional nonlethal confrontation ends on explicit yield.

Resolution: A limited consensual illusion, a public truth with social costs, or a negotiated mixed space can result. No single machine destruction automatically decides what everyone should believe.
